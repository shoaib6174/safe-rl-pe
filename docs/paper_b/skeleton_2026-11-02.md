## Overview (the paper's flow in one paragraph)
A learned pursuer under a CBF projection filter stops moving and never moves again: the filter blocks the commanded
action, the body stops, the observation freezes, the memoryless policy re-emits the same command — a closed-loop fixed
point at which the safety guarantee holds, violations are zero and the task fails silently (Fig. 1). We characterise the
fixed point (Prop. 1: the known KKT/normal-cone condition for CBF-QP deadlock, applied to a learned memoryless policy
with three learning-side refinements), measure why learning does not remove it (frozen input; the escape's reward
arrives ~630 steps later, worth 0.009 at γ = 0.99; Fig. 2), show that every learning-side remedy we pre-registered
improves the task but leaves the failure in place while a stall penalty makes it worse (Figs. 3, 5), and show that the
minimal stateful shield — one counter, candidates drawn from the same admissible set — clears the deadlock on 3/3
policy families, raises capture on 9/9 seeds, keeps zero violations (Prop. 3, Figs. 3–4), is insensitive to its one
parameter over 5–80 steps, leaves traps that a random-perturbation baseline cannot leave at all (capture higher 5/5), and
composes onto a second filter class 4/4 (Figs. 6–8, B1–B4; D159/D160). Three pre-registered dissociations show that filter-adjacent liveness metrics can
improve while the task collapses, so safety-filter papers that report violations alone can miss the failure entirely.

#### 1. Introduction (~750 words)
**Purpose:** state the failure, why standard metrics miss it, and the four contributions.
- 1.1 Safety filters (CBF-QP, shields) wrap learned policies with a forward-invariance guarantee [Hsu–Hu–Fisac 2024;
  Ames et al. 2019; Alshiekh et al. 2018; Cheng et al. 2019]. The guarantee is about the *safe set*, not about progress.
- 1.2 The observation (Fig. 1): a trained pursuer, filtered, stalls for 1 106 steps commanding full speed with
  σ = 0.000 while executing zero; the same episode with the recovery captures at step 262. Zero violations either way.
- 1.3 Why this is not the multi-robot deadlock story: single agent vs static geometry; the trap is in observation space
  (the policy's input freezes), not a force equilibrium between agents [Grover et al. 2020/2023]; nearest neighbours
  are the undesired equilibria of CBF-QPs [Reis–Aguiar–Tabuada 2021; Tan–Dimarogonas 2024], which concern the
  closed loop of a *fixed nominal controller*; ours is what happens when the nominal is *learned through* the filter.
- 1.4 Contributions: (C1) characterisation of filter-induced stalls for learned memoryless policies, with position
  traps vs pair traps, eventual constancy, and a stochastic dwell law, measured (Prop. 1, B1); (C2) the mechanism
  that defeats learning — frozen input and a discount horizon ≈ 6× too short — measured on 772 stall entries (S15);
  (C3) a pre-registered comparison in which every learning-side remedy improves the task and none removes the
  failure, a stall penalty backfires, and a one-counter forward-offering recovery removes it with no change to the
  guarantee (S16/S17, Prop. 3, B2), composing onto a second filter class (B3/B4); (C4) three pre-registered
  dissociations between filter-adjacent liveness metrics and the task (S16/S18/S19) — a measurement warning.
- 1.5 One sentence on protocol: 7 pre-registered rounds, frozen commits, paired seeds, records and code released.
**Transition:** the related work places C1 against the deadlock literature and C3 against conservativeness fixes.

#### 4. Why a filtered memoryless policy stops (~900 words; the formal section)
**Purpose:** a short, honest formal core: one characterisation that is known, three refinements that are ours, one
remark that is a bound, one proposition about the remedy.
- 4.1 **Prop. 1 (stall characterisation).** For the exact tier, the filter is the projection P_A(s) onto the convex
  admissible set A(s) in the weighted norm. The executed speed is zero iff the nominal u_π(o(s)) lies in the
  (weighted) normal cone of A(s) at the zero-speed face (Grover et al.'s KKT condition; Tan–Dimarogonas for the
  single-agent QP). Proof sketch: projection onto a convex set; stationarity. **Refinements (ours):** (R1) *position
  trap vs pair trap*: a stall at (p, θ) can be a filter trap at heading θ while some heading at p admits forward
  motion — rotation in place is not a fixed point of the state, only of the position; the pair (policy, filter) is
  what is trapped. (R2) *eventual constancy*: with the policy's input o(s) a function of the pose, the opponent's
  visibility and bounded filter features (the stall-run feature saturates at 80 steps), the input becomes constant
  after ≤ 80 steps whenever the opponent is out of view, so the closed loop is at a fixed point *of the observation*
  from then on; this is why filter-state features alone (RC19) recover only part of the loss. (R3) *stochastic dwell*:
  for a Gaussian policy, the per-decision leave probability is the policy's mass whose filtered, held action
  displaces the body; the dwell is geometric with that parameter. B1 does NOT support it (D159: the long stalls are the
  intercept law's, which has no sampling noise; where the head is in control the observed dwell is 3–12× shorter than
  predicted) → R3 is demoted to a remark that deadlock survives exploration (0.24–0.57 vs 0.20–0.55, 4/4).
  (R4) *two kinds of zero*: the nominal may itself be zero (self-stop) — the same fixed point reached by a policy that
  learned not to ask (the penalty mechanism of §6). Corollary: violations are zero along the loop; safety metrics are
  blind to it. Scope: exact tier (B1: 100 % of stalled states; pair trap 98 %; the exact projection moves in 2 % — the
  enumeration's share, stated), deterministic environment, memoryless policy.
- 4.2 **Remark (why learning does not remove it).** The escape's advantage must attach to one shared input vector
  (R2) and is discounted: A_γ ≤ γ^T · A_0 with the measured T ≈ 629 steps and A_0 = 0.257 (S15), i.e. 0.0005–0.009
  at γ = 0.99 and still only 0.137 at γ = 0.999 (Fig. 2c). This is an upper bound on a learning signal, not a proof
  of failure; the GAM arm (γ = 0.999) is the test: capture up, deadlock not cleared (§5.2).
- 4.3 **Prop. 3 (the recovery).** After N consecutive immobile steps, candidates (v_max, 0), (½ v_max, 0), the nominal,
  (0, ±ω_max) are filtered in order and the first that moves is executed; else the larger admissible rotation. (a)
  Every executed command is an output of the same filter on the same state, so the forward-invariance guarantee is
  exactly the base filter's (no new assumption; H-SAFE in every round). (b) If the position is not a position trap,
  the body typically leaves it within N + T_turn steps (T_turn = 45 at ω_max): rotate-then-move (B2: 97.8 % of 10 475
  triggers at N = 20, BASE-62 92–95 %, the tail reported; D160). (c) Caveat: a stall–recover–stall
  cycle is not excluded by (a)–(b); it is measured (B2: cycles per engagement 0.08–0.56, immobile-step rate 0.02–0.10
  at N = 20; D160). The anti-spin
  test of S17 was uninformative and is reported as such.
- 4.4 Positioning sentence: any minimal-deviation shield (incl. Kim et al.'s closest-safe-action) satisfies Prop. 1's
  hypothesis; the remedy is the minimal state (one counter) that breaks R2 without changing A(s).
**Transition:** §5 measures each clause.

#### 6. Three dissociations: liveness metrics vs the task (~450 words)
**Purpose:** the measurement warning, each instance pre-registered (Fig. 5).
- 6.1 S16 PEN: stall-step rate halves while immobile-step rate doubles and capture falls (the agent stops asking).
- 6.2 S18: the correction-magnitude penalty of Pizarro Bejarano et al. (RA-L 2025, VERIFIED; they square it, we use the
  unsquared norm in normalised units; it helped in their trajectory-tracking task where stopping is never rewarding)
  improves the deadlock metric in 9/9 cells while capture falls 3/3 (pooled p 3.6e-51); zero-motion requests rise to
  0.37–0.80. Case-5 confound stated (penalty charged for the law's interventions too).
- 6.3 S19: law pins −94…−99 % with capture flat or worse; the pin was a symptom.
- 6.4 Consequence: report the task; report immobility AND stalled separately; report firings per episode for any
  recovery; a deadlock metric that improves under a penalty is a red flag, not a result.

#### 7. Discussion, limitations, and what we did not show (~400 words)
- Memory: the recurrent arm was confounded (S16-A4) → memory is untested; the claims are about memoryless policies.
- One environment, one dynamics model, two filter constructions; policies are 5 M-from-scratch (S16/S17) and 10 M
  (STR14/RC19); three seeds per family; N = 500 paired.
- The discount remark is a bound; GAM shows the horizon is implicated and insufficient.
- Scripted-law stalls (B1 law-branch share) are the same fixed point and are not "learning failures".
- Recovery cost: cycles and rotation time (B2); the cheapest alternative (PERT) cannot leave a heading trap (D160).
- Process: pre-registration did not catch the loop; a human watching the behaviour did. Fifteen specifications
  passed while measuring an artefact (D125). Behavioural review is part of the protocol since.
- Practical recommendation (one paragraph): keep the filter; add the counter; log firings; watch the episodes.
