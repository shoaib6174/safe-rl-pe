- **D3 argumentative_coherence — block.** Contribution C2 ("the mechanism that defeats learning") and the sentence "every learning-side remedy improves the task" (abstract; §5.2 p. 7) are contradicted or undercut by the paper's own data: 97–100% of the Base policies' stalled states and 57% of Aware's arise on the scripted law branch with the evader out of view (supplement Table B1 "Law" column; `results/derisk/s26_b1_round.json` → `classification.*.law_branch_share`; per-cell `visible` = 0 at every law-branch stalled state), so a more parsimonious explanation of "learning does not remove it" — the stalled command is not emitted by learned parameters — fits the data better than the discount argument and is not addressed; the penalty arms and the recurrent arm lower capture.

- **D5 writing_and_structure — warn.** The manuscript is evaluable and within the page limit; Figure 5 (six panels at 0.68 of the text width) and the annotation text of Figure 1 (0.46 width) are legible only with magnification, and Experiment 3's registered primary metric is never named, so the reader cannot trace "the rotation-only recovery scored 0.000 deadlock episodes" to the registered verdict ("No arm solves the loop, REC included").

## Review Body

### Devil's Advocate Review

**What the paper does well.** It records failed hypotheses in the registered direction and says so in print (Experiments 3, 5, 6, 7, 9 all carry failed bars), it archives every number with the record it came from, and the recovery result itself (deadlock 0.19–0.30 → 0.000 with zero violations, Base capture 0.37–0.47 → 0.44–0.72, Experiments 4 and 8) is real, reproducible from the archive, and useful to anyone who has put a projection filter in front of a controller.

#### Strongest Counter-Argument

The paper's title, abstract, Section 4 ("Why a filtered memoryless policy stops") and Remark 2 tell a learning story: a memoryless learned policy sees a frozen observation, the escape's reward is 629 steps away and worth 0.009, so learning cannot find the way out. The archive tells a different one. The pursuer is a structured controller: a scripted intercept law that chases while the evader is visible, then drives to a remembered last-seen point when it is not (`pe2/structured.py`, `InterceptLaw.last_seen`, "it is the scripted law's state"); the PPO head is consulted only when the law "has nothing to act on". Experiment 7's own classification records that 100%, 97%, 100% of the three Base policies' stalled states and 57% of Aware's were produced on the law branch (supplement Table B1), and in every one of those states the evader was out of view, so the stalled command came from the last-seen branch — a scripted controller carrying memory, driving at an obstacle between it and a remembered point. The manuscript describes this law as acting "when it is visible" and calls both parts "memoryless" (p. 3). Both statements are wrong for exactly the states the paper is about.

Once that is seen, the learning claims lose their footing. "Learning does not remove it" is true for the trivial reason that the stalled command is not emitted by learned parameters; the discount bound of Remark 2 is not needed to explain it and, when its own prediction is tested (γ = 0.999 lifts the signal to 0.137, above the paper's 0.05 bar), the Hor family still deadlocks in 0.29–0.61 of episodes — more than Base. The Obs, Hor and ObsHor families change how often the law is handed a bad approach, not what happens at the stall, which is why they move capture and not deadlock. The residue is sound and modest: a projection filter in front of a fixed nominal, scripted or learned, reaches the normal-cone fixed point that Reis, Tan and Grover already described; a counter with a rotation candidate leaves it; the authors' own Experiment 4 record calls the remedy "an engineering one". That paper is honest and publishable. This one borrows a learning mechanism it did not isolate.

#### Logic-chain validation (Proposition 1 → recovery → dissociations)

1. *Proposition 1* is nominal-agnostic and correct as stated; it does not require the nominal to be learned, memoryless, or anything but a fixed function of the current input. Its second sentence ("if o(s) is constant along the stall … absorbing") is definitional for a deterministic map. The chain is sound here but carries no learning content.
2. *R2 "eventual constancy"* is the only step that invokes the policy class, and it is where the chain bends: constancy of the input is needed for a learned head; the law branch does not need it, since its target point is remembered and unchanged. The paper's data show the law branch is where the long stalls live (p. 4 R3: "the long stalls belong to the intercept law").
3. *Remark 2* is advertised as a bound and is not one (M1); its direct test (Hor) fails to clear the deadlock, which the paper reads as "horizon implicated and lengthening it insufficient" (p. 10). The reading that the horizon is irrelevant at a law-branch stall is not considered.
4. *Proposition 3 and Experiments 4, 8, 9* follow from Proposition 1 alone and hold regardless of C1. This part of the chain is intact.
5. *The three dissociations* (§6) each hold as reported, but the third (Exp. 6) is in tension with the thesis (M4): removing the law's stalls left the task flat.

So: the geometric and engineering halves survive; the learning half is unsupported by the evidence as presented.

#### Cherry-picking and confirmation-bias checks across the nine experiments

- Numbers quoted in text match the archived records in every cell I checked (Exp. 3 `s16_round.json`; Exp. 4 `s17_round.json.gz`; Exp. 6 `s19_round.json`; Exp. 7 `s26_b1_round.json`; Exp. 8–9 `s26_b2_round.json`). Failed bars are printed (H-B1-3, H-B1-4, H-B2-2, H-B2-4, H-B3-1, H-PLAN-1/2, H-FIX-1). No favourable-subset selection of seeds or cells was found.
- Confirmation bias operates at the level of *reading*, not reporting: (a) Exp. 6's registered verdict ("the law was NOT the binding constraint") becomes a dissociation in favour of the thesis; (b) Exp. 3's registered failure ("No arm solves the loop, REC included") becomes "the rotation-only recovery scored 0.000 deadlock episodes … hence forward candidates come first"; (c) the perturbation baseline's foreseen failure becomes evidence for the rotation candidate (M2); (d) the Hor family's failure to clear deadlock becomes support for the horizon mechanism; (e) "Fifteen specifications passed their bars" (p. 10) is offered as a lesson about watching episodes, when it is equally a statement that the protocol section (p. 5) describes a method that did not find the paper's own phenomenon. Each individual reading is defensible; the direction of all five is the same.
- Pre-registration integrity: the Experiments 7–9 pre-registration was committed about ten hours before the outcome and amended three times before launch, once "round-changing", all before any record existed. That is pre-registration as advertised. The prior rounds' amendments (S16-A4, recorded after launch but before numbers) are disclosed in the pre-registration files. No issue beyond m10.

#### Overgeneralisation and the "so what" test

The result a practitioner takes away is: a projection filter can stop a body and a counter with a rotation candidate restarts it. Every roboticist who has shipped a CBF or MPC filter has a watchdog; the paper's own Experiment 4 record calls the remedy "an engineering one" (`s17_round.json.gz` → `summary.direction`). What would make the paper matter beyond the watchdog is the learning claim — that training through the filter cannot fix this and why — and that is the claim C1 removes from the evidence. The dissociation finding (liveness metrics improve while the task collapses) is the second thing that could matter, and it is real for the two penalty experiments. The paper should be built on those two legs, not on "learned memoryless policies".

The generality question (one game, one dynamics model, two filters) is answered by the authors in §7 and is fine as a scope statement; it is not fine in the title, in the "Any filter … Kim et al." sentence, or in the prediction that Proposition 1 explains other papers' timeouts (M5).

#### Answers to the authors' questions

(a) **Proposition 1: result or restatement?** Restatement, by the paper's own words ("The characterisation is known"). The second sentence is definitional. The genuine addition is R1 (pair trap vs position trap, measured: 98% vs 0%), which is a measurement, not a proposition. Recommend: state the normal-cone condition as a Lemma with citation, and state R1 as the measured claim; keep Proposition 3.

(b) **Does the nine-experiment protocol section earn its space?** The protocol paragraph (p. 5, eight lines) is cheap and should stay. Table 1 earns its space only if the branch column from C1 is added to it (which policy branch produced the stalls each experiment counts). Experiments 1 and 5 could move to the supplement without loss to the argument.

(c) **Figures 1, 3, 6, 7 at printed size.** Fig. 1 (0.46 width): the traces read; the in-panel text ("stalled 1 106 steps: v_nom = 1.000 ± 0.000") and the legend will be ~5 pt — move the annotation to the caption. Fig. 3 (0.72 width): panels read; the legend block under (a) floats free of its panel — put it inside (a). Fig. 6 (0.46 width): two simple panels, legible. Fig. 7 (0.6 width): panel (a)'s x-category labels and (c)'s legend are at the limit. The worst offender is not on your list: Fig. 5 (six panels at 0.68 width) is not legible in print; give it the full width or cut to four panels.

(d) **Do the three dissociations belong?** Two do (stall penalty, correction-magnitude penalty): they are the paper's most transferable finding. The third (Exp. 6) belongs only after M4 is answered; as written it argues against the thesis more than for it.

(e) **Naming.** Experiments 1–9, Free/Aware, G/H/A are clear. Base/Obs/Hor/ObsHor are clear once defined (p. 3) but the definition arrives after Table 1 uses them; move it above the table. "The intercept law" is not clear at all, because it is mis-described (C1).

(f) **Do the claims stay within the evidence?** No, on three points: the learned/memoryless framing (C1), "every learning-side remedy improves the task" (M3), and the generalisation to other filters and papers (M5). Yes on the recovery's deadlock, capture, violation and composition claims, which are exactly what the records show.

### Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths

1. **The stalled command is scripted, not learned** (C1). More parsimonious than the discount mechanism for "learning does not remove it", and it explains why Obs/Hor/ObsHor move capture without moving deadlock and why the Hor test fails.
2. **A random resolution that accepts rotations.** The obvious baseline for "is the structured candidate list necessary?"; the one run (M2) cannot rotate by construction.
3. **A recurrent policy with matched rollout settings.** The paper says memory is untested; the title still says "learned policies". A single matched re-run of the recurrent arm (evaluation of three seeds, 5 M steps each) would settle whether the claim is about memoryless policies or about this controller.
4. **Fixing the filter rather than adding a counter.** Tan and Dimarogonas (2024), already cited, modify the QP to remove undesired equilibria while keeping forward invariance; the paper's choice to "keep the filter" is stated but the comparison is not run, so "cheap to remove" is relative to one alternative only.
5. **Longer predictive horizons.** Horizon 4 (0.2 s) is the shortest predictive filter one could build; the loop already weakens under it (2 of 4 at the bar). A horizon sweep would say whether the phenomenon is a projection-filter property or a short-horizon property.
6. **Training with the recovery in the loop.** The recovery is applied at evaluation to frozen policies; a policy trained through filter + recovery might stop needing it (ObsHor already fires 363 times vs Base's 152 675), which would turn the engineering patch into a training recommendation.

### Missing Stakeholder Perspectives

- The hardware operator: 5–89% of firings at N = 20 are rotations and one policy fires 100 times per episode; nobody speaks for actuator wear, spinning in front of people, or what a rotating robot looks like to a certifier.
- The certifier: Proposition 3(a) says the guarantee is the base filter's; whether a stateful shield outside the filter changes the certified artefact is not discussed.
- The adversary designer: the learned attacker that induces the stalls is treated as fixed; whether it can re-learn to exploit the recovery's cycles is not asked.
- The RL architect: the paper restricts to memoryless policies after one confounded recurrent arm; the audience that would use memory is not addressed.

### Unexamined Premise

That "the task fails" *because of* the stall. Experiment 6 removed 94–99% of a policy's law-held stalls and the task did not move; Experiments 4 and 8 removed stalls and the task rose by 0.17–0.35. The paper never states the condition under which a stall costs the task, so "the guarantee holds and the task fails" is asserted as a general coupling that the paper's own data show to be policy-dependent.

### Observations (Non-Defects)

- Free + recovery captures 0.728 against greedy, equal to Free without the filter (0.728, Exp. 1). That is the cleanest number in the paper: for that policy the filter's entire task cost was the stall. It deserves a sentence.
- The authors' pre-registration discipline is unusually good; the records carry what is needed (`branch`, `visible`) to repair C1 without a new run.
- "In practice: keep the filter, add the counter, log the firings, watch the episodes" (p. 10) is the paper's honest contribution and would survive every issue above.

## Editorial Decision

**Major Revision.** Contract failure condition F1 fires (D3 block; D1, D2 also warn, so F2 fires as well); within F1's disjunction I choose Major Revision rather than Reject because the central empirical results (Experiments 4, 7, 8, 9) reproduce from the archive and the defects — the mis-described controller and its memory, the learning mechanism attributed to a branch that does not learn, a mis-derived bound, a baseline that cannot rotate, two reframed registered verdicts, and over-general claims in the title and p. 5 — can be repaired in one cycle by re-describing the setup, re-scoping the contribution, splitting every headline number by branch, and running one rotation-accepting baseline.
