Contract: `reviewer/reviewer_full/v1` (mode reviewer_full, panel 5). Metadata only: title "The Guarantee Holds and the Task Fails: Absorbing Stalls of Learned Policies under CBF Filters"; field: safety-critical control for learned policies / safe reinforcement learning, pre-registered simulation study with two propositions; about 3 400 words of main text, 10 PMLR pages including eight figures and one table, submitted to L4DC 2027. No manuscript file has been opened.

## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 methodology_rigor (mandatory).** From the adversarial seat, this dimension asks whether the study design would survive a hostile replication attempt: whether the experimental units, seeds, episode counts, test statistics and pre-registration are reported in enough detail that a sceptic could redo the work and could also see what was *not* reported. A pre-registered simulation study advertises a high bar for itself; my job is to check that the bar is met rather than merely invoked, that failed or deviating hypotheses are disclosed with the same prominence as confirmed ones, and that the artefacts (records, code, commits) actually let a reader test for selective reporting.

**D2 domain_accuracy (mandatory).** This dimension asks whether the claims are true relative to what the field already knows: whether prior work on filter-induced stalls, undesired equilibria and shielding is represented correctly, whether the formal statements (two propositions) are correct as stated, and whether terminology from control (barrier functions, forward invariance, filters) and from reinforcement learning (discount, policy class, observation) is used precisely. The adversarial question is whether a result presented as new is a restatement of something known under another name, and whether a prior work is described in a way that flatters the paper's delta.

**D3 argumentative_coherence (mandatory).** This is the centre of my role: does the chain from premise to central claim hold at every link, and is the central claim the one the title and abstract promise? I will look for a linchpin whose removal collapses the argument, for hidden assumptions, for a more parsimonious rival explanation that fits the same data, for data-conclusion mismatches inside the paper's own tables and figures, and for a gap between what was measured and what is concluded.

**D4 cross_disciplinary_relevance (high).** The paper sits between safety-critical control and reinforcement learning; the adversarial reading asks whether a reader from either side can follow the definitions and would accept the implications, and whether any claim that bridges the two fields (for example that a control-side guarantee interacts with a learning-side property) is substantiated rather than asserted. Overgeneralisation beyond the studied setting is the failure pattern I expect to probe here: the title is general, and the burden is on the evidence.

**D5 writing_and_structure (normal).** Organisation, clarity, figure and table legibility at printed size, and adherence to the venue's conventions (page limit, naming consistency, placement of material in the supplement). For the Devil's Advocate this is the lowest-weight dimension; I will note defects that impede a first-time reader's ability to check the argument, and will not inflate presentation issues into substantive ones.

## Scoring Plan

### D1: methodology_rigor
- what_to_look_for: explicit statement of the experimental unit, number of seeds and episodes per condition, paired or unpaired comparisons, the statistical test and its assumptions, pre-registration status of each hypothesis (registered before or after the data), disclosure of hypotheses that failed or were revised, availability of archived records and frozen code that would let me test whether quoted numbers are a favourable subset, and consistency between numbers in text, table, figures and archive.
- what_triggers_block: evidence of selective reporting that changes a central conclusion (quoted seeds or cells are a favourable subset of what was archived, or a failed pre-registered test for a central claim is omitted or recast as success); a central claim resting on a comparison with no stated N, no test, or a test misapplied so badly the conclusion does not follow; or an unreproducible pipeline for the central result (no records, no code, no commits).
- what_triggers_warn: pre-registration asserted but not checkable; small seed counts or few conditions used for strong claims without an explicit scope limit; multiplicity across many hypotheses unaddressed; numbers inconsistent between text and figures or archive in a non-central place; deviations from the registered plan disclosed only in the supplement or only in passing.

### D2: domain_accuracy
- what_to_look_for: whether each proposition is stated with its hypotheses and whether the proof or proof sketch carries them; whether the stall or deadlock phenomenon is positioned correctly against existing closed-loop analyses of safety filters; whether terminology (barrier, invariance, filter, admissible set, discount, memoryless policy) is used in the field's sense; whether cited works are described accurately and the paper's delta from them is stated honestly.
- what_triggers_block: a proposition that is false as stated or whose proof does not establish the claim used later in the argument; a central novelty claim that is a known result misrepresented as new; or a factual misstatement about prior work that the paper's contribution depends on.
- what_triggers_warn: a proposition that is correct but trivially so and is presented as a result; a delta from prior work that is asserted rather than demonstrated; imprecise use of a technical term that leaves a claim ambiguous; a missing closely related line of work whose inclusion would change how the contribution is framed but not whether it stands.

### D3: argumentative_coherence
- what_to_look_for: the single sentence that states the central claim, and every link from evidence to that sentence; a linchpin result whose removal collapses the argument; the presence of a simpler rival explanation (an engineering fix, a known equilibrium, an artefact of the setup) that the paper does not confront; internal contradictions between sections; whether the recovery or remedy proposed follows from the diagnosis or is independent engineering; whether the paper's own narrative of how the failure was found is consistent with what it claims about its protocol.
- what_triggers_block: the main conclusion does not follow from the presented evidence even if the evidence is valid; the paper's own data contradict a stated conclusion; or a rival explanation is both more parsimonious and a better fit for the presented data and is not addressed.
- what_triggers_warn: a logical leap that is plausible but unsupported at one link; a rival explanation that is acknowledged but dismissed without evidence; a central claim phrased more strongly in the title or abstract than the body supports; a contribution that depends on an untested assumption the paper names but does not test.

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance
- what_to_look_for: definitions accessible to both control and learning readers; whether claims that bridge the fields (guarantee on one side, learning behaviour on the other) are substantiated by experiments that vary the relevant factor; the scope of generalisation in the title and abstract versus the number of tasks, dynamics models, filters and policy classes actually studied; whether the implications offered to each community follow from the evidence.
- what_triggers_block: an interdisciplinary claim central to the paper (for example that a class of filters produces a class of learning failures) that is asserted with no evidence varying the relevant factor, or that is contradicted by the paper's own scope; or framing so field-specific that one of the two target audiences cannot evaluate the central claim at all.
- what_triggers_warn: generalisation in the title or abstract beyond the studied setting without an explicit scope statement; a bridging claim supported by a single setting or a single mechanism; definitions that one audience would need to look up with no pointer given.

### D5: writing_and_structure
- what_to_look_for: whether a first-time reader can map the naming of experiments, policies and opponents without external records; figure legibility at the printed column width; whether the page budget is spent on what the argument needs; whether material pushed to the supplement is material the main text's claims depend on; venue conventions respected.
- what_triggers_block: the manuscript cannot be evaluated as submitted (figures unreadable at the size that carries a central result, naming so inconsistent that claims cannot be traced to evidence, or page limit violated in a way the venue would desk-reject).
- what_triggers_warn: figures or tables that are legible only with magnification; naming that requires the reader to hold a crosswalk in mind; a section whose length is out of proportion to its contribution to the argument; essential supporting detail only in the supplement.
