## Review Body

### Reviewer Focus
Can the nine pre-registered experiments, with three seeds per family and 500 paired episodes per cell, support each numbered claim at the stated N and p; are the metrics valid constructs; are failed hypotheses reported as such; and does the released archive let a reader verify every number.

### Overall Recommendation
Major Revision (derived mechanically from F2; see Editorial Decision). The revision is a text revision: no new experiment is required for any issue below, and the archive already contains everything needed to fix them.

### Summary Assessment
The paper reports a pre-registered simulation study of a failure mode — a CBF-filtered memoryless learned pursuer stalls at a closed-loop fixed point while the safety guarantee holds — and a one-counter recovery that removes it. Methodologically this is unusually careful work: hypotheses, bars and the sentence to write on failure were fixed before data; code was frozen at a named commit per experiment; evaluation seeds are paired across arms on fresh streams; binary outcomes are compared with the exact two-sided binomial on discordant pairs; construct gates reproduce shared cells bit for bit; every number I traced is in the archive, and the per-episode indicators let me recompute the headline p-values to three significant figures. Failed bars are reported as failed in most places (H-B1-3's 99 %, H-B1-4, H-B2-2, H-B2-4, H-B3-1). The weaknesses are in reporting, not design: one headline quantity (the 629-step "delay to capture") is a different construct from what it is called; two registered verdicts are omitted while the text promises all of them; the "pre-registered dissociations" are post-hoc readings; no multiplicity policy or interval is given; Figures 6 and 7 are not legible at their printed size. Under the contract, three mandatory warns force Major Revision; in substance the fixes are a few sentences, one relabelled quantity, two wider figures and a verdict column in Table 1.

### Strengths

**S1 — Pre-registration that is auditable, not asserted.** The protocol paragraph (p. 5) is backed by `docs/pathway/*.md` in the release, each hashed into the record's provenance (`spec_sha256`), with amendments dated and marked "before any number exists" (e.g. S16-A4, S26 A2/A3). The round records carry the verdicts per hypothesis, so a reader can check that "the registered bar was 99 %" (p. 6) is literally H-B1-3's bar.

**S2 — The right test for the design, reproducible from the archive.** Paired episodes are tested with the exact binomial on discordant pairs (b, c), not with an unpaired test on means. From `s26_b2_cells/` I recomputed Free 0.426 → 0.728 as b = 151, c = 0, p = 7.01 × 10⁻⁴⁶ and Aware 0.560 → 0.654 as b = 47, c = 0, p = 1.42 × 10⁻¹⁴; both match the text (p. 7). The seven-of-eight and the all-p < 0.05 claims of Exp. 9 (p. 8) also reproduce (max p = 0.0017 and 2.8 × 10⁻⁴ respectively).

**S3 — Operational metric definitions with thresholds.** §3 (p. 3) fixes immobile (≤ 0.05 v_max), stalled (immobile with nominal ≥ 0.5 v_max), self-stop, deadlock episode (run ≥ 40 steps), the three trap notions, escape (0.10 m), engagement and cycle. The stalled/self-stop split is what makes the Exp. 3 and Exp. 5 readings possible at all, and the paper uses it consistently.

**S4 — Failed and uninformative tests are mostly reported as such.** The dwell test (p. 6), the escape bound's tail (p. 7), the perturbation baseline's registered expectation that failed in the opposite direction (p. 8), the predictive-filter bar met on two of four (p. 8), the confounded recurrent arm (p. 6) and the uninformative anti-spin bar (p. 7) are all stated plainly. This is the standard the rest of the paper should be held to (see W2).

**S5 — Reproducibility artefacts above the field's norm.** Frozen commits per experiment, checkpoint SHA-256 in each cell record, seed streams, the name→identifier crosswalk (supplement Tables 2–3 and README), per-episode indicators and escape times in every cell, and bit-identity gates (CG-B2-1 on twelve shared cells).

### Weaknesses

**W1 — "Delay from escape to capture ≈ 629 steps" is not that quantity.** *Problem.* Remark 2 (p. 4) defines T as "the delay from the escape to the capture" and sets T ≈ 629; the abstract says "the reward for escaping arrives 629 steps later"; C2 says "about 630 steps later". The archived field is `mean_steps_remaining_escaped = 628.59`, the mean number of steps left in the episode after an escape over all 97 escaped hold-10 entries, 68 of which ended in a 1 200-step timeout. On the 29 entries that escaped and were captured, the mean delay to capture is 346 steps (median 314); on the 8-cell pool it is 384. Figure 2(b)'s caption says "delay from escape to capture" while its axis says "steps from escape to episode end" and its histogram pools 208 escapes (mean 677), not the 383-entry hold-10 set the text quotes. *Why it matters.* The number is in the abstract and in a Remark that presents a bound; with T = 346 the bound at γ = 0.99 is 0.0079, not 0.0005 (still below the 0.05 bar, so the conclusion survives, but the headline figure is off by a factor of 17 and the construct is wrong). *Fix.* Either rename the quantity throughout ("mean remaining horizon after an escape") and state that 70 % of escapes still end in timeout, or recompute T on captured-after-escape entries (346) and report both. Make Figure 2(b)'s caption, axis and pool agree with the text. The measured discounted advantage 0.0092 is the right construct and should carry the argument. *Severity: Major.*

**W2 — Two registered verdicts are missing from a paper that promises all of them.** *Problem.* (i) Exp. 2's registered P1 ("the escape advantage is negligible — |Δ discounted return-to-go| < 0.05 and capture after escape < 0.15") is recorded as FAILS, with the registered direction "the signal was there and learning still missed it; the explanation is optimisation, not credit assignment". The paper reports the numbers (p. 6) but not the verdict, and reads them as "the escape's reward lies beyond the training horizon". (ii) Exp. 5's primary hypothesis H-CROSS predicted that the correction penalty *raises* capture in the dense column; capture fell on three of three seeds and the registered direction reads "Hypothesis B is REFUTED and the paper does not exist in this form". The paper reports only the dense column of eighteen arms and omits that SPARSE showed no pooled effect (p = 1.0) and DRARE a non-significant one (p = 0.13). *Why it matters.* §5 states "Every verdict below is the registered one, including the failures" (p. 5); a reader auditing the archive will find two failures that the text does not name, which damages exactly the credibility the protocol section buys. The P1 direction is also a substantive counter-reading of C2 that the authors themselves registered. *Fix.* One sentence each: "P1 failed as registered: the discounted clause held (0.0092 < 0.05) but capture after escape was 0.299, not < 0.15; the undiscounted signal exists and is discounted away" — which is in fact what Remark 2 argues; and "H-CROSS failed: the penalty lowered capture in the dense column instead of raising it, and had no effect in the sparse and rare columns (supplement)". Add the SPARSE/DRARE rows to the supplement. *Severity: Major.*

**W3 — "Pre-registered dissociations" are post-hoc readings.** *Problem.* C4 and §6 (pp. 2, 9) call the three dissociations pre-registered. What was registered in each case was the measurement set and a hypothesis that failed: H_FIX_1 (PEN lowers immobility — it rose), H-CROSS (above), H-PLAN-1 (planner raises capture — it did not). The dissociation reading ("liveness metric improves while the task collapses") was composed afterwards. The sentence "Each of the following pre-registered hypotheses failed in the registered direction" (p. 9) is also ambiguous: for Exp. 5 the failure is in the *opposite* direction to the registered prediction. *Why it matters.* Pre-registration earns credibility only for what was pre-specified; relabelling an exploratory pattern as registered is the HARKing red flag the authors' own protocol exists to prevent. *Fix.* "Three dissociations observed in pre-registered measurements" and, per item, name the registered hypothesis and its verdict before giving the reading. The section loses nothing by this and gains the honesty the rest of the paper has. *Severity: Major.*

**W4 — No multiplicity policy, no intervals, no power statement.** *Problem.* I count on the order of sixty paired tests across the nine experiments (Exp. 1: 12 cells + 3 remedies × 6 comparators; Exp. 3: 6 families × 3 seeds; Exp. 4: 7 × 3; Exp. 5: 3 columns pooled + 9 cells; Exp. 6: 3; Exp. 8: 25 + 10; Exp. 9: 8 + 4), all at a nominal p < 0.05 with no stated family-wise policy. No rate difference is given an interval (the archive has them: `s4_round.json` h1 ci = [−0.375, −0.289]). No a priori power or minimum-detectable-difference statement is made for N = 500 paired. *Why it matters.* Most p-values are below 10⁻⁵ and would survive any correction, so the headline claims are safe; but the marginal ones are load-bearing where the paper argues "flat": Exp. 6's +0.026 (p = 0.015), −0.018 (p = 0.035), −0.022 (p = 0.007) would not survive a Bonferroni family of ~60 (threshold 8 × 10⁻⁴), and GAM-61's capture gain (p = 0.0068) and the Exp. 9 BASE-60/GREEDY deadlock contrast (p = 0.0017) sit near it. *Fix.* State the policy in the Protocol paragraph (pre-specified primary per experiment, no correction within the registered family, and name which secondary p-values are fragile); add 95 % Newcombe/Wilson paired-difference intervals for the headline deltas (Exp. 1, Exp. 4 Base seeds, Exp. 8 Free/Aware); one sentence on the minimum detectable difference at N = 500 paired given typical discordance. *Severity: Major (statistical reporting), easily repaired.*

**W5 — Free and Aware are selected artefacts, and Aware's training is under-described.** *Problem.* §3 says Aware was trained "behind it with the filter's state in its observation" (p. 3). The S5 pre-registration shows RC = shield observation **and** stall penalty 1 × 10⁻³, and RC19 was chosen by the registered artefact rule "among passing arms, the seed with the highest gate rate". Exp. 1 (p. 5) reports RA (features only) and RB (penalty only) but not the RC arms (0.564, 0.550, 0.444) from which Aware was taken. *Why it matters.* Aware is one of four or five policies in Experiments 7, 8 and 9; a best-of-three selected on the same opponent inflates its baseline and is a different object from the three-seed families. The omission of the penalty also matters because §6 argues that a stall penalty teaches the agent to stop asking. *Fix.* State in §3 that Free and Aware are single checkpoints selected as the best seed on an earlier gate (and the rule), and that Aware was trained with features and a 10⁻³ stall penalty; report the RC arms in the Exp. 1 paragraph. *Severity: Minor to Major (disclosure).*

**W6 — Small numerical over-statements.** (i) Abstract: "clears the deadlock on three of three policy families (0.19–0.30 to 0.000)": 0.19–0.30 is the Base family only (Obs 0.26–0.41, ObsHor 0.26–0.38) and ObsHor with the recovery is 0.002–0.006, not 0.000. (ii) p. 7 "The median escape takes 4 to 8 steps at every N": per-cell medians at N = 40 are 4–10 and at N = 80 4–9 (`H-B2-2.all`). (iii) p. 6 "the evader is visible in 2.4 % of stalled steps" is the hold-1 pool figure (0.0235); the hold-10 pool the sentence sits in gives 3.4 %. (iv) "p ≤ 8 × 10⁻⁴" (p. 5) rounds 8.11 × 10⁻⁴ downward. *Fix.* Rewrite (i) as "(Base 0.19–0.30 to 0.000; Obs to 0.000; ObsHor to ≤ 0.006)"; (ii) "4 to 10"; (iii) state the pool; (iv) "≤ 9 × 10⁻⁴" or exact. *Severity: Minor.*

### Detailed Comments

**Research questions and hypotheses.** Clear and answerable; each experiment maps to named hypotheses with bars. The paper would be stronger if Table 1 carried a "registered verdict" column (PASS/FAIL per hypothesis) so the reader sees the fifteen-plus verdicts in one place instead of inferring them from prose.

**Research design.** Appropriate: frozen policies, paired seeds across arms, scripted and learned opponents, an ablation structure (Base/Obs/Hor/ObsHor) that isolates observation and discount, a parameter sweep, a random-perturbation baseline that is the standard alternative, and a second filter class. Internal validity is high; external validity is limited to one game, one dynamics model and two filters, which §7 states. One design limitation the paper should name: the K = 40 deadlock indicator is also the trigger-adjacent quantity for the recovery at N = 20 (a recovery that fires at 20 immobile steps cannot by construction leave a 40-step stall run unless it fails), so "deadlock 0.000" at N ≤ 20 is partly definitional; the stall-run-at-80/160 figures the authors give for N ≥ 40 are the right instrument and should also be given for N = 20 (they are in the archive: 0.000–0.014).

**Sampling and seeds.** Three seeds per family with per-seed reporting is the right minimum and the authors never pool seeds into a family rate for inference. The "nine of nine" and "three of three" statements are counts of per-seed tests, which is correct; the text should say once that three seeds support a consistency claim, not a rate. Evaluation seeds are fresh streams distinct from training (README table); good.

**Data collection.** Episode length, opponents, hold = 10, obs_width and checkpoint hashes are recorded per cell. PPO hyperparameters (network, learning rate, n_steps, batch, entropy, 5 M steps) are not given in the paper or supplement; the code holds them, but a reproducibility paragraph should list them.

**Analysis methods.** Exact binomial on discordant pairs is correct for paired binary outcomes and assumption-light (independence across episodes is given by fresh seeds). Where rates are continuous (immobile-step rate, stalled-step rate) no test is applied and the paper correctly refrains from "significant"; it should refrain equally from "halves" (p. 9) without an interval. Censoring of undecidable triggers is handled per amendment A3 (reported: 94 censored of 10 560) but "decidable" is not defined in the paper; add half a sentence. The dwell prediction in Exp. 7 (10/p_leave) could be tested on only two policies; the paper says so.

**Statistical reporting adequacy (Step 4a).** Descriptives: rates and N given per cell (good); effect sizes: rate differences given, no standardised measure needed for binary paired data; CIs: absent; power: absent; assumptions: satisfied by design; missing data: censoring stated in archive, not paper; APA format: p-values given in scientific notation, acceptable for the venue. Red flags: many tests without a stated policy (medium); a post-hoc framing labelled pre-registered (W3, high until fixed); no p-hacking signals (stop rule, bit-identity gates, frozen commits). Completeness: **Adequate (≈ 72/100)**; becomes Exemplary with W4 fixed.

**Results presentation.** Numbers in text, captions and figure JSON agree except where noted in W1 and W6. Figures show per-seed points (good) but no intervals; Figure 4-left's trailing lines from bare to recovered seed are informative. The figure JSON `figure_data_s26.json` reports `escape.n_decided = 10466` while the round record and the paper give 10 475; the archive should agree with itself.

**Construct validity of the metrics.** *Deadlock episode* (K = 40) is explicit and its insensitivity for N ≥ 40 is acknowledged. *Immobile* counts rotation; the paper learned this (the anti-spin bar) and says the bar was uninformative — good, but then "immobile-step rate falls from 0.594 to 0.071" (p. 7) should be read with the firings per episode beside it, which the paper does. *Stalled* requires nominal ≥ 0.5 v_max, so a policy emitting a zero nominal exits the metric — the paper exploits exactly this in §6 and defines *self-stop* to catch it; consistent. *Cycle*: "an engagement that ends in a stall within 40 steps of the previous one" — previous engagement or previous stall? Disambiguate. *Trap at heading* is measured on a grid of the command box (n_admissible_grid = 21 in F6a); say the grid resolution in the paper since "no admissible command" is a grid statement. *Pair trap* equals trap-at-heading minus position-trap and is therefore 98.1 % by construction once position traps are 0 %; the paper should not count it as a separate finding.

**Reproducibility.** Strong (S5). Gaps: repository URL and corresponding email are placeholders; license pending; `s17_round.json.gz` is gzipped and "nothing reads it" per README — state how to regenerate Fig. 4 from the `s17_arm_*.json` files, which the figure script does; PPO hyperparameters absent from text; the "277 firings per episode" (p. 7) for the rotation-only recovery is not locatable in the summary records I inspected (REC-60 totals 47 205 firings over 1 500 episodes, consistent with a heavy tail) — give the record and field.

**Methodological fallacies checked.** Survivorship/selection: W5 (selected artefacts) — disclose. HARKing: W3 — relabel. Uncorrected multiple comparisons: W4 — state policy. Confirmation bias: largely avoided; the paper reports its own failed bars, with the two omissions of W2. Circularity: deadlock metric vs recovery trigger (see Research design) — give the longer-run metric at N = 20 too. No ecological, Simpson's, or reverse-causation concerns.

### Answers to the authors' questions

**(a) Is Proposition 1 a result or a restatement?** As stated, a restatement: the first sentence is the KKT/normal-cone condition the paper itself attributes to Grover et al. and Tan & Dimarogonas ("The characterisation is known", p. 4), and the second sentence ("if o(s) is constant along the stall, the closed loop is at a fixed point") is immediate from the definitions. The *result* is the measured scope — on 262 stalled states, 98.1 % traps at heading, 0 % position traps, projection agreeing with the filter in 98.1 % — together with R1 and R2. My suggestion: label the statement "Proposition 1 (discrete-time projection form of the CBF-QP deadlock condition; restated)" or make it a Lemma, and state the measured scope as the numbered result ("Observation 1" or fold it into Proposition 1 as a measured clause with its bar and verdict). Reviewer 2 should judge the formal precision.

**(b) Does the nine-experiment protocol section earn its space?** The Protocol paragraph (10 lines, p. 5) earns it; it is the paper's warrant. What does not yet earn its space is the absence of a verdict table: the reader is told verdicts are registered but must collect them from prose. Replace nothing; add a "registered hypotheses / verdict" column to Table 1 (or a compact supplement table) and the multiplicity sentence of W4. If space must be found, Exp. 1's second half (the RA/RB numbers) and the dwell-prediction detail of Exp. 7 can be shortened.

**(c) Are Figures 1, 3, 6, 7 legible at printed size?** Figure 1 (0.46 linewidth): yes, the annotation text is small but readable; consider dropping the top-panel annotation into the caption. Figure 3 (0.72, three panels): marginal — panel (b)'s two-line category labels and the legend under (a) are at the limit; the figure needs the full text width or panel (c) moved to the supplement (it reports a failed test the text already states). Figure 6 (0.46 linewidth, two panels): **no** — axis labels and tick labels are below 5 pt at print; use the full width or stack the panels. Figure 7 (0.60, three panels): **no** — panel text is too small and in (c) the legend ("capture, line law / law held at a stall, line law") overlaps the y-axis tick region (visible as "0.6law" in the extracted text); give it the full width and move the legend outside the axes. None of the eight figures carries an interval; at minimum Figures 4-left, 5(b) and 6(b) should.

**(d) Does the three-dissociations section belong in this paper?** Methodologically, yes if and only if it is relabelled as what it is (W3): three post-hoc readings of pre-registered measurements whose registered hypotheses failed, each introduced by that hypothesis and its verdict. In that form it is a useful warning to practitioners that follows from R4 and the stalled/self-stop split. In its current "pre-registered dissociations" form it is the one place where the paper's framing exceeds its protocol, and I would rather see it shortened than keep that label. Exp. 5's two other columns belong in the supplement with their null results.

**(e) Is the naming clear to a first-time reader?** Experiments 1–9: clear, but they appear in the text in the order 1, 2, 7, 3, 4, 8, 9, 5, 6; say once that numbering follows the logical order of Table 1, not the order of discussion. Base/Obs/Hor/ObsHor: clear and self-explanatory. Free/Aware: adequate once §3 is read, but "Aware" under-describes the policy (W5). G/H/A: clear from Table 1's caption, but the single letter A collides with A₀ (Remark 2) and 𝒜(s) (Proposition 1); write "the attacker" in prose and keep A for the table.

**(f) Do the claims stay within the evidence?** The numbers do: 20 of 23 spot-checks match the archive exactly and the headline p-values recompute from per-episode indicators. The claims that step past the evidence are: the 629-step "delay to capture" (W1, a construct error on a headline number); "three pre-registered dissociations" (W3); the abstract's "(0.19–0.30 to 0.000)" across three families (W6-i); "every learning-side remedy improves the task" (Hor seed 3's gain is p = 0.62, and the penalty and recurrent arms worsened it — "every remedy that adds information or horizon" would be accurate); and "insensitive to N over 5–80" in the abstract, which is true for capture and for the longer-run stall metric but not something the K = 40 indicator can show for N ≥ 40 (the body says so; the abstract should say "capture insensitive to N").

## Editorial Decision

**Major Revision.** Failure condition F2 fired (D1, D2 and D3 each score warn; no dimension blocks). The reason is reporting, not design or evidence: the archive supports the paper and the headline tests reproduce independently, but the manuscript (i) labels a mean remaining horizon as a delay to capture in the abstract and in Remark 2, (ii) omits two registered verdicts after promising all of them and (iii) calls post-hoc readings pre-registered, while giving no multiplicity policy or intervals. Each fix is textual and the authors already hold the numbers; with W1–W4 addressed and Figures 6–7 widened I would expect to recommend acceptance on re-review.
