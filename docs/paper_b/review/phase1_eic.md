## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory).** From the editor's chair this dimension asks whether the study, as designed and reported, would survive the scrutiny of the venue's methodology-minded reviewers: is the experimental design adequate to the questions posed, are data handled and statistics reported to the standard of a learning-and-control venue, and does the paper offer enough reproducibility affordances (code, records, seeds, protocol) that a reader could check it. Because the metadata announces a pre-registered simulation study, I will hold the paper to the standard it sets itself: pre-registration should be visible, and the reporting should let the reader tell confirmed from failed predictions. I will not re-derive statistics (that is Reviewer 1's work); I judge whether the overall design is sound and whether the reporting is honest and complete at the level a 10-page venue paper permits.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory).** This dimension asks whether the paper's claims sit correctly within the current literature on safety filters, control barrier functions, shielding and safe reinforcement learning: prior work must be represented fairly, terms of art used correctly, and results stated without factual error. For an editor the centre of gravity is originality and positioning: whether the paper knows the closest prior work on filter-induced stalls and undesired equilibria, cites it, and states its own delta accurately rather than by omission. Any formal statement (the metadata mentions two propositions) must be labelled for what it is — a new result, a specialisation, or a restatement — and the field's existing conditions must not be presented as new.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory).** This dimension asks whether the paper's thesis is one thesis, consistently stated from title to conclusion, and whether each claim is carried by evidence presented in the paper. The title promises a strong framing ("guarantee holds, task fails"); the dimension asks whether that framing is earned by what is shown, whether the formal part and the empirical part actually connect, and whether the conclusions avoid over-generalising from the setting studied. I will look for gaps between what the experiments can show and what the text asserts, for hedges that appear only in the limitations, and for any inference that needs an untested premise.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high).** L4DC's readership spans control theorists, learning researchers and roboticists. The dimension asks whether a reader from either side (a CBF theorist who does not train policies; an RL researcher who has not read the deadlock literature) can follow the framing, definitions and implications without the authors' private vocabulary, and whether any claim that crosses the boundary (e.g., a statement about what learning can or cannot repair, or about how a control-theoretic condition manifests in a learned policy) is substantiated rather than asserted. Internal naming conventions and compressed protocol descriptions are the usual failure point here.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal).** This dimension asks whether the manuscript is well organised, clearly written, whether figures and the table are legible and informative at printed size, and whether the paper respects the venue's conventions (PMLR format, 10-page main-text limit, supplement use, anonymisation and AI-use statements as required). With eight figures and one table in 10 pages and about 3 400 words, the risk is that figures are too small to read or that sections compete for space; I will judge whether the allocation of space serves the argument.

## Scoring Plan

### D1: methodology_rigor
- dimension_id: D1
- what_to_look_for: an explicit statement of the pre-registration (what was predicted, where it is recorded, whether the record predates the data); a clear experimental protocol with numbers of seeds, episodes and conditions; paired or otherwise appropriate comparison design; statistical tests named and matched to the data type; effect sizes or raw rates reported alongside tests; failed or null predictions reported as such; a reproducibility artefact (repository, archived records, frozen versions) referenced from the text; limitations that acknowledge the design's scope (one simulator, one dynamics class, number of filters).
- what_triggers_block: the pre-registration is claimed but no record or protocol is identifiable; results are reported without seed counts or sample sizes so that no claim can be checked; the design cannot in principle distinguish the paper's hypothesis from an obvious alternative (confound built into the comparison); evidence that numbers in the text do not match the paper's own tables, figures or supplementary records; reproducibility affordances asserted but absent.
- what_triggers_warn: seed counts or sample sizes are small relative to the number of comparisons and this is not discussed; tests are reported without effect sizes or intervals; some predictions' outcomes are omitted or only partly reported; protocol details necessary to read the results are deferred to the supplement without pointer; the repository is referenced but its contents are not described well enough to know what it reproduces.

### D2: domain_accuracy
- dimension_id: D2
- what_to_look_for: correct use of safety-filter and CBF terminology (forward invariance, safe set, QP filter, deadlock/undesired equilibrium, shielding); the closest prior work on filter-induced deadlock and undesired equilibria cited and characterised accurately; the paper's formal statements labelled correctly relative to known conditions; the safe-RL and shielding literature on training through filters and on filter-induced behavioural change represented fairly; no claims about prior methods that misstate what they do; bibliography entries that correspond to real, verifiable publications.
- what_triggers_block: a central formal statement is presented as new when it restates a known condition without acknowledgement; a key prior line of work on filter-induced stalls is absent and the paper's novelty claim depends on that absence; a factual mischaracterisation of a cited method or result that the paper's positioning relies on; a fabricated or unverifiable reference carrying weight in the argument.
- what_triggers_warn: closest prior work is cited but its relation to the paper is stated too briefly or too favourably to the paper; terminology drift (the same object called by different names, or a field term used loosely); a formal statement whose status (result / corollary / restatement) is left ambiguous; one or two relevant references missing that would qualify but not undermine the positioning.

### D3: argumentative_coherence
- dimension_id: D3
- what_to_look_for: a single, stable statement of the thesis across title, abstract, introduction and conclusion; a logical chain from the formal part to the experiments to the conclusion with each link evidenced; claims scoped to the setting studied; the framing in the title carried by the evidence rather than by rhetoric; limitations that are consistent with the claims (not contradicting them); no reliance on an untested premise for a central inference; the proposed remedy's contribution argued, not assumed.
- what_triggers_block: the conclusion asserts something the experiments did not test (an impossibility or universality claim without the supporting design); the title/abstract thesis differs materially from what the body shows; an internal contradiction between stated results and stated conclusions; a central inference that is a non sequitur.
- what_triggers_warn: the thesis is stated with different strength in different sections; over-generalisation that is hedged in the limitations but not where the claim is made; a section whose contents do not serve the central argument and dilutes it; the remedy's significance claimed beyond the comparisons presented.

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance
- dimension_id: D4
- what_to_look_for: definitions of the key objects on first use, accessible to both control and learning readers; an explicit statement of why a control audience and a learning audience should each care; naming of experiments, policies and opponents that a first-time reader can hold in mind; boundary-crossing claims (what learning can/cannot repair; how a control condition appears in a learned policy) backed by evidence or a cited result; implications stated for practitioners of both communities.
- what_triggers_block: the paper is unreadable to one of the two communities because essential definitions or protocol are missing from the main text; an interdisciplinary claim central to the paper is unsubstantiated (asserted about learning with no learning evidence, or about control with no control argument).
- what_triggers_warn: a naming scheme that requires a lookup table to follow; definitions present but scattered or late; implications for one community stated while the other is left to infer them; a boundary-crossing claim supported only partially.

### D5: writing_and_structure
- dimension_id: D5
- what_to_look_for: clear sectioning with a protocol section proportionate to its role in a 10-page paper; figures legible at printed width (axis labels, legends, line weights, panel labels), each with a caption that states what to see; the table readable and referenced; prose free of internal jargon; compliance with PMLR/L4DC conventions (page limit, supplement, required statements, placeholders resolved or flagged); consistent notation.
- what_triggers_block: the main text exceeds the venue's page limit or violates a hard submission rule; figures unreadable to the point that results cannot be checked from them; organisation so disordered that the argument cannot be followed.
- what_triggers_warn: several figures below comfortable legibility at printed size; a section that takes space disproportionate to its contribution; placeholders or formatting defects left in the submission; notation inconsistencies; captions that do not state the takeaway.
