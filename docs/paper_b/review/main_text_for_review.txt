                   Policies under CBF Filters

                                                        Abstract
     A safety filter that projects a learned policy’s command onto an admissible set guarantees forward
     invariance of the safe set, and nothing about progress. We show that a control barrier function
     (CBF) filter in front of a memoryless learned pursuer creates a closed-loop fixed point at which the
     guarantee holds, violations are zero, and the task fails silently: the filter zeroes the command, the
     body stops, the observation freezes, and the policy re-emits the same command for the rest of the
     episode. We state the fixed point as the normal-cone condition for CBF-QP deadlock applied to a
     learned nominal and measure it: 98% of stalled states are traps at the current heading, none is a trap
     of the position. We measure why learning does not remove it: the reward for escaping arrives 629
     steps later and is worth 0.009 at γ = 0.99. Across nine pre-registered experiments, every learning-
     side remedy improves the task and none removes the failure, a stall penalty makes it worse, and
     a one-counter recovery that offers filtered forward and rotation candidates after N immobile steps
     clears the deadlock on three of three policy families (0.19–0.30 to 0.000), raises capture on nine of
     nine seeds with zero violations, is insensitive to N over 5–80 steps, and composes onto a second
     filter. Three dissociations show liveness metrics improving while the task collapses.
     Keywords: safety filters; control barrier functions; shielding; safe reinforcement learning; dead-
     lock; liveness; pursuit–evasion


1. Introduction
Safety filters wrap a learned policy with a guarantee. A control barrier function (CBF) filter, a shield,
or a predictive filter takes the policy’s command, tests it against a safe set, and replaces it with the
nearest admissible command when the test fails (Hsu et al., 2024; Ames et al., 2019; Alshiekh et al.,
2018; Cheng et al., 2019). The guarantee is forward invariance of the safe set; it says nothing about
the task, and the two can come apart in a way no violation count reveals.
    Figure 1 shows the failure in one archived episode. A PPO-trained pursuer behind a CBF filter
drives toward an obstacle between it and the evader; the filter zeroes its forward command. The
body stops, the observation stops changing, and the memoryless policy emits the same full-speed
command at each of the next 1 106 steps (standard deviation 0.000) while the executed speed stays
at zero. The episode ends in a timeout. The same episode under the recovery of Section 4, same
seed and same evader, ends in a capture at step 262. The violation count is zero in both.
    This is not the multi-robot deadlock of the CBF literature, a force equilibrium between agents
whose constraint normals cancel the nominal (Grover et al., 2020, 2021, 2023); here one agent
stands against static geometry. The nearest prior objects are the undesired equilibria that CBF
quadratic programs introduce into the closed loop of a fixed nominal controller (Reis et al., 2021;
Tan and Dimarogonas, 2024). Ours is a nominal trained through the filter that sees the world only
through an observation the stall has frozen: the trap is in observation space, and that is why learning
does not escape it.

2. Related work
Deadlock and undesired equilibria of CBF-QP controllers. Grover et al. (2020, 2021, 2023)
characterise multi-robot deadlock under CBF quadratic programs through the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker
conditions: the projected command is zero exactly when the nominal lies in the conic hull of the
active constraint normals. Reis et al. (2021) showed that CLF–CBF programs create spurious stable
equilibria on the unsafe set’s boundary for a single agent and one obstacle; Tan and Dimarogonas
(2024) characterised all closed-loop equilibria and modified the program to remove the undesired
ones while keeping forward invariance; Reis and Aguiar (2026); Reis et al. (2025) extended the
analysis, the latter noting that liveness is not guaranteed. These works fix the filter; we keep it and its
guarantee untouched, show that a learned nominal makes the equilibrium absorbing in observation
space, and remove it with a stateful shield outside the filter.

Shield conservativeness. Kim et al. (2025) require that a continuous-space shield always has
a safe action to offer, including stateful requirements such as loop avoidance, and note that the
shield alone is overly conservative; Dawood et al. (2025) adapt an MPC shield’s weights with a
learned supervisor; Wang et al. (2025) resolve multi-robot deadlocks after they occur; Arnström
and Teixeira (2025) study adversarial filter deactivation. Our remedy is one counter, evaluated on
learned policies in an adversarial game; Proposition 1 predicts the oscillations and timeouts these
papers report.

Learning through the filter. Oh et al. (2026) show that a filter-unaware policy chatters against
the filter and that training through it is optimal when the filter is permissive enough; Wu et al.
(2026) train through a filter for minimally exploitable game policies; Georgescu et al. (2026) repair
a shield’s specification online to keep liveness achievable; Pizarro Bejarano et al. (2025) penalise
the filter’s correction during training. We measure the case in which training through the filter still
fails: at the stall the filter is not permissive, and the signal that would teach the escape is discounted
away. We found no prior work that separates filter-adjacent liveness metrics from task success under
pre-registration (Section 6).

Filter. The base filter is a discrete-time CBF projection: a command is admissible if the exact
one-step barrier condition holds on the true Euler successor, and the filter executes the admissible
command nearest to the nominal in the weighted norm wv (∆v)2 + wω (∆ω)2 (wv = 150, wω = 1),
with barrier rate 0.2 and a virtual control point 0.1 m ahead of the axle; it has exact, relaxed and
backup tiers, and every stalled state in this paper lies on the exact tier. The second filter (Section 5.3)
is a sampling lookahead through the simulator with horizon 4. A violation is the simulator’s contact
flag; the safety hypothesis requires zero contact steps in every filtered cell.
Definitions. A step is immobile if the executed speed is at most 0.05 vmax ; stalled if immobile
while the nominal speed is at least 0.5 vmax ; a self-stop if immobile and not stalled. A stall run is a
maximal sequence of stalled steps; a deadlock episode contains a stall run of at least K = 40 steps
(2 s). A state is a filter trap at heading if no admissible command at the current pose moves the
body (speed above 0.05 vmax ), a position trap if this holds at every heading there, and a pair trap if
it is the former and not the latter. An escape is a displacement of 0.10 m from the stalled pose; an
engagement is a cluster of recovery firings fewer than five steps apart; a cycle is an engagement that
ends in a stall within 40 steps of the previous one.
Policies and experiments. Table 1 lists the experiments. The BASE, O BS, H OR and O BS H OR
families (three seeds each) are trained from scratch for 5 M steps behind the filter; O BS adds the
filter’s admissible-turn features to the observation, H OR raises the discount to 0.999, O BS H OR does
both. F REE and AWARE are 10 M-step policies from the experiments in which the failure was first
seen: F REE was trained without the filter, AWARE behind it with the filter’s state in its observation.
All policies are frozen before evaluation.

4. Why a filtered memoryless policy stops
Let s be the pursuer’s state, o(s) its observation, uπ = π(o(s)) the nominal command, and A(s) ⊂
[0, vmax ] × [−ωmax , ωmax ] the set of commands that pass the exact one-step barrier test at s: the
command box intersected with the half-spaces of the linearised barrier rows, hence convex. On

                                                          3
                                           S HOAIB S IDDIQUE




the exact tier the filter executes PA(s) (uπ ), the unique projection of the nominal onto A(s) in the
weighted norm.

Proposition 1 (Stall characterisation) On the exact tier the executed speed is zeroP    if and only if
uπ lies in the weighted normal cone of A(s) at the zero-speed face, equivalently uπ = j∈J(s) µj aj
with µj ≥ 0 over the active barrier normals aj . If moreover o(s) is constant along the stall, the
closed loop is at a fixed point and the stall is absorbing for the rest of the episode.

Proof [Proof sketch] Projection onto a closed convex set returns the u⋆ with ⟨uπ −u⋆ , u−u⋆ ⟩W ≤ 0
for all admissible u; on the zero-speed face this is the normal-cone condition, and stationarity of
the Lagrangian gives the conic combination of active normals, the force-equilibrium condition of
Grover et al. (2023) for one agent and static constraints. If o(s) is constant, so are uπ , the projection,
and s.

    The characterisation is known; the learned setting adds four refinements, measured in Section 5.
(R1) Position trap versus pair trap. A stall at pose (p, θ) can be a trap at heading θ while another
heading at p admits forward motion; what is trapped is the pair (policy, filter): the policy asks for
the blocked direction, and the filter admits only commands the policy never asks for. (R2) Eventual
constancy. The observation depends on the pose, the opponent’s visibility, and bounded filter fea-
tures whose stall counter saturates at 80 steps, so with the opponent out of view the input is constant
after at most 80 stalled steps; filter-state features (the O BS family, AWARE) therefore move the fixed
point without removing it. (R3) Exploration. One might expect a Gaussian head’s stall to end once
a sampled action displaces the body, with a geometric dwell. Experiment 7 does not support this:
the long stalls belong to the intercept law, which samples nothing, and where the head is in control
the evader’s motion ends the stall sooner than sampling would; what survives is that deadlock un-
der stochastic sampling is no rarer than under deterministic actions. (R4) Two kinds of zero. The
nominal may itself be zero; a policy that has learned not to ask reaches the same fixed point as one
whose request is blocked (Section 6).

Remark 2 (Why learning does not remove it) Let A0 be the undiscounted capture advantage of
escaping a stall and T the delay from the escape to the capture. By (R2) the advantage attaches to
one shared input vector, and under the training discount it is at most γ T A0 . Experiment 2 measured
A0 = 0.257 and T ≈ 629 steps: 0.0005 at γ = 0.99 (measured discounted advantage 0.0092)
and 0.137 at γ = 0.999. This bounds a learning signal; it does not prove failure. The H OR family
(γ = 0.999) is the test: it raises the task and does not clear the deadlock.

The recovery. After N consecutive immobile steps, the shield filters the candidates (vmax , 0),
( 21 vmax , 0), the nominal, and (0, ±ωmax ) in that order and executes the first whose filtered speed
exceeds 0.05 vmax ; if none moves, it executes the larger admissible rotation. The counter resets on
any mobile step.

Proposition 3 (Recovery) (a) Every command the recovery executes is an output of the base filter
on the current state, so the forward-invariance guarantee is exactly the base filter’s. (b) If the stalled
position is not a position trap, some heading admits forward motion; the admissible rotation reaches
it within Tturn = 45 steps at ωmax , after which the forward candidate passes, so the body typically
leaves the position within N + Tturn steps. (c) Nothing in (a)–(b) excludes a stall–recover–stall
cycle; it is measured, not bounded.

5. Experiments
Protocol. Each experiment was specified before any number existed: hypotheses, bars, verdict
rules, and the sentence to write if a hypothesis failed. Code was frozen at a commit, cells used the
same seeds across arms on fresh streams, and paired differences were tested with the exact two-sided
binomial test on discordant pairs at p < 0.05. Construct gates refuse to summarise an experiment
until shared cells reproduce the earlier records bit for bit and designated animated episodes have
been watched; a stop rule forbids re-running or re-cutting a cell after its number exists. Every
verdict below is the registered one, including the failures.

     Experiment 2 measured the mechanism on 772 stall entries (Figure 2). On the pre-registered

each with deterministic actions and the same seeds with stochastic sampling (Figure 3; per-policy

was seen in a rule-based pursuer in a separate game. Self-stops are 41 to 66% of the BASE policies’
immobile steps and absent for AWARE.

5.2. Remedies

rotation-only recovery. Observation features and a longer horizon lowered immobility and raised
capture without clearing the deadlock; the recurrent arm was confounded by an amendment to its
batch settings and licenses no conclusion about memory; the stall penalty backfired (Section 6).

6. Three dissociations: liveness metrics versus the task
Each of the following pre-registered hypotheses failed in the registered direction (Figure 7).

A stall penalty teaches the agent to stop asking. In Experiment 3 the penalty family, trained
with a stall penalty, halves the stalled-step rate (0.143–0.249 to 0.093–0.158) while its immobile-
step rate rises from 0.436–0.448 to 0.633–0.833 and its capture against the greedy evader falls from
0.384–0.478 to 0.204–0.348. The policy learns to emit a zero nominal (R4); the fixed point is the
same.

A correction-magnitude penalty improves the deadlock metric and hurts the task. Experi-
ment 5 trained 18 arms with the penalty of Pizarro Bejarano et al. (2025) on the magnitude of the
filter’s correction (they square it; we use the unsquared norm in normalised units, calibrated to an
expected episode penalty of one). In their quadrotor tracking task the penalty raised return and re-
duced chattering; standing still is never a local optimum there. In the dense-reward column capture
falls on three of three seeds (0.442, 0.480, 0.528 to 0.338, 0.300, 0.212; pooled p = 3.6 × 10−51 )
while zero-motion requests rise from 0.07, 0.16, 0.00 to 0.37, 0.78, 0.80, and the pre-registered
deadlock metric improves in nine of nine cells (one seed from 0.224 to 0.106 while its immobile-

Removing the pins removes a symptom. Experiment 6 replaced the intercept law with a planner

stalled steps separately, and firings per episode for any recovery; a liveness metric that improves
under a penalty is a warning, not a result, until the task moves with it.

7. Discussion and limitations
The claims are about memoryless policies; the one recurrent arm was confounded before its number
existed, so memory is untested. One game, one dynamics model, two filter constructions; three
seeds per family, 500 paired episodes per cell. Remark 2 is a bound: the longer-horizon family
shows the horizon is implicated and lengthening it insufficient. The intercept law’s stalls are the
same fixed point as the learned head’s.
    The recovery has a cost: cycles are 8 to 56% of engagements at N = 20, the escape bound fails
on 2.2% of triggers, and the firing count varies by three orders of magnitude across policies that
capture equally well. A random perturbation that accepts only moving commands cannot leave a
trap at heading; one that accepted rotations would be a different baseline, not run here.
    Fifteen specifications passed their bars while measuring the artefact this paper is about; the
failure was found by a person watching the animated episodes (1 085 consecutive blocked requests,
command standard deviation 0.002, an unregistered diagnostic of 100 episodes). Watching the
behaviour is now a construct gate in our protocol. In practice: keep the filter, add the counter, log
the firings, watch the episodes.

8. Conclusion
A minimal-intervention safety filter in front of a memoryless learned policy can create a closed-
loop fixed point at which the guarantee holds, violations are zero, and the task fails silently. We
characterised it, showed that its geometry is the admissible set’s, measured why the learner cannot
see the way out, and showed across nine pre-registered experiments that learning-side remedies im-
prove the task without removing the failure while a one-counter recovery from the same admissible
set removes it with the guarantee untouched. The failure is cheap to remove and easy to miss; the
second point is the one we would like this paper to change.
