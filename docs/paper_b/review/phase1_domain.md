Metadata available: title "The Guarantee Holds and the Task Fails: Absorbing Stalls of Learned Policies under CBF Filters"; field: safety-critical control for learned policies / control barrier function filters, pre-registered simulation study with two propositions; about 3 400 words of main text, 10 PMLR pages with eight figures and one table, submitted to L4DC 2027. ## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 (methodology_rigor, mandatory).** From a domain-accuracy standpoint I read this dimension as: does the study design let the formal statements and the simulation evidence support each other? For a paper that pairs propositions with a pre-registered simulation study, the design must make the modelled object (the filter, the dynamics, the policy class) match what the propositions describe, must expose the assumptions under which the formal statements hold, and must give enough detail (filter definition, dynamics, discretisation, archived records) that a control-theory reader could re-derive the conditions and, in principle, re-run the experiments. I will defer the statistical machinery itself to Reviewer 1 and judge only whether the design is adequate to test the control-theoretic claims.

**D2 (domain_accuracy, mandatory).** The paper's statements about control barrier functions, safety filters, forward invariance, deadlock/undesired equilibria and liveness must agree with the established literature; prior results must be attributed and characterised correctly (neither claimed as new when known, nor misdescribed); terminology (CBF, QP filter, forward invariance, deadlock, equilibrium, discount horizon, absorbing set) must be used in the field's accepted sense; and any formal statement labelled a proposition must actually be true under its stated hypotheses with a proof or proof sketch that is sound. This is my primary dimension.

**D3 (argumentative_coherence, mandatory).** The thesis announced in the title (the safety guarantee is preserved while the task fails because of absorbing stalls) must be carried by a chain in which the formal characterisation, the mechanism explanation, and the empirical measurements all point to the same object; claims about what can or cannot remove the failure must be bounded by what was tested or proved; and the limitations stated must match the limitations that actually exist. I will look for leaps between "we observed this in one setting" and "this is a property of CBF filters on learned policies".

**D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance, high).** L4DC readers come from both learning and control. Definitions must be stated so that a learning reader can follow the control content (what a barrier test is, why projection matters) and a control reader can follow the learning content (what a memoryless policy, a discount factor, an observation space are); claims that bridge the two (for instance, that a learning-side property interacts with a control-side filter to produce a failure) must be substantiated rather than asserted.

**D5 (writing_and_structure, normal).** Organisation, clarity of the formal exposition, legibility of the eight figures and the table at PMLR column width, and adherence to L4DC/PMLR conventions (page limit, notation discipline, proposition/remark numbering, reference completeness). Lower priority, but a formal result stated with ambiguous notation is a writing failure with domain consequences, and I will flag it under D5 when the mathematics is recoverable and under D2 when it is not.

## Scoring Plan

### D1: methodology_rigor
- dimension_id: D1
- what_to_look_for: an explicit statement of the filter (constraint, objective, decision variable, discrete- or continuous-time), the dynamics, and the policy class that the propositions address; a check that the simulated filter is the same object the propositions describe; pre-registration that names hypotheses and metrics before the data; archived records and code that would allow a reader to reproduce the filter and the deadlock measurements; limitations that acknowledge what the single simulation setting does not cover.
- what_triggers_block: the formal object analysed in the propositions and the object simulated are materially different and the paper does not acknowledge the gap (e.g. a continuous-time CBF condition is proved while a discrete heuristic is run, with conclusions transferred without comment); or the central empirical claim rests on a measurement whose definition is absent or incoherent so that no reader could reproduce it.
- what_triggers_warn: assumptions needed for the propositions (differentiability, feasibility, time step, bounded inputs, uniqueness of the projection) are left implicit; the experimental setting is narrow relative to the generality claimed and the narrowing is under-stated; reproducibility material is promised but not specified; a design choice that affects the control-theoretic conclusion (filter margin, class-K gain, discretisation) is fixed without justification or sensitivity.

### D2: domain_accuracy
- dimension_id: D2
- what_to_look_for: the propositions' hypotheses and conclusions written out precisely enough to check; proof sketches that use the right objects (KKT conditions or normal cones for projection-type filters, invariance arguments for forward invariance); correct positioning against the known CBF-QP deadlock and undesired-equilibria results; correct use of "deadlock", "equilibrium", "forward invariance", "liveness", "absorbing"; correct attribution of CBF, safety-filter, and shielding foundations; no mis-stated prior results in the bibliography or the text; whether any "bound" is derived or merely asserted.
- what_triggers_block: a proposition that is false as stated, or whose proof sketch does not establish its conclusion and the gap is load-bearing for the paper's thesis; a known result presented as the paper's own without attribution; a factual mis-statement of CBF theory or of a cited paper that the central argument relies on.
- what_triggers_warn: a proposition that is true but imprecisely stated (missing hypotheses, ambiguous quantifiers, notation that admits two readings) where the intended reading is recoverable; a positioning claim relative to prior deadlock work that overstates the novelty without being false; a remark labelled as a bound that is really a heuristic estimate, if the text does not say so; missing key references in the deadlock/liveness line when the paper's novelty claim depends on their absence; terminology drift that does not change the result.

### D3: argumentative_coherence
- dimension_id: D3
- what_to_look_for: a traceable chain from the formal characterisation through the proposed mechanism to the empirical tests and the proposed remedy; whether the remedy's justification follows from the same characterisation; whether negative claims ("X cannot remove the failure") are scoped to the tested or proved cases; whether the conclusion and abstract claim neither more nor less than the body; internal consistency of numbers, names and definitions across sections.
- what_triggers_block: the central thesis is contradicted by the paper's own evidence or by its own propositions; or the remedy's claimed property (e.g. that it preserves the guarantee) is asserted in a way that the presented argument does not support and the paper's conclusion depends on it.
- what_triggers_warn: an unscoped generalisation from the tested setting to the class of CBF filters or learned policies; a claimed impossibility that rests on an argument the paper itself labels approximate; a section whose findings do not feed the main argument and whose presence weakens the chain by diluting it; inconsistencies between abstract, body and conclusion that a careful reader can resolve.

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance
- dimension_id: D4
- what_to_look_for: definitions of the control objects (barrier function, filter, invariance) and the learning objects (policy, observation, discount, horizon) given where first used; an explanation of why a learning-side property should interact with the filter that a control reader can verify; implications stated for both audiences; a reader without access to internal records able to follow the experimental naming.
- what_triggers_block: a bridging claim (learning property X causes filter failure Y) that is the paper's central contribution and is neither proved nor empirically isolated, leaving adjacent-field readers unable to tell whether the effect is real.
- what_triggers_warn: definitions assumed from one side without statement; implications stated for only one audience; naming or notation that an adjacent-field reader must reverse-engineer; the interdisciplinary mechanism asserted with partial evidence and labelled as established.

### D5: writing_and_structure
- dimension_id: D5
- what_to_look_for: a formal section whose notation is consistent and whose propositions are numbered and referenced; figures legible at PMLR width with readable axis labels and legends; a table that carries its information without the text; a protocol/experiments section proportioned to its contribution within 10 pages; references complete and consistently formatted; prose free of undefined acronyms.
- what_triggers_block: a formal statement that cannot be parsed because of notation collisions or missing definitions, with no recoverable intended meaning; or a page/structure violation of the venue that would cause a desk rejection.
- what_triggers_warn: figures whose essential content (lines, labels, legends) cannot be read at printed size; sections disproportionate to their argumentative weight; notation that changes meaning between sections; naming schemes introduced without a key; references with missing fields.
