# Phase 1 — Peer Reviewer 1 (Methodology), paper-content-blind pre-commitment

Contract: `reviewer/reviewer_full/v1` (mode reviewer_full, panel 5). Metadata only: title "The Guarantee Holds and the Task Fails: Absorbing Stalls of Learned Policies under CBF Filters"; field: safety-critical control for learned policies / safe reinforcement learning; pre-registered simulation study with two propositions; about 3 400 words of main text, 10 PMLR pages including eight figures and one table; submitted to L4DC 2027. No manuscript file has been opened.

## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 methodology_rigor (mandatory).** From the methodology seat this is the dimension I own. The question is whether the study design can answer the questions the paper poses, whether the data handling and the statistical reporting meet what a careful experimental-robot-learning reviewer expects (pre-registered hypotheses with pass/fail criteria stated before the data; paired designs analysed as paired; sample sizes and seed counts justified or at least honestly bounded; tests matched to the data type; effect sizes and uncertainty reported alongside p-values; multiplicity acknowledged when many hypotheses are tested), and whether the paper gives a reader the artefacts needed to reproduce it (code, records, frozen versions, a crosswalk from names in the text to records). For a pre-registered simulation study the extra bar is that the registration is auditable: the registered hypotheses, the stopping rules and the analysis plan should be visible, and deviations declared. Two propositions imply a formal component whose assumptions must be stated precisely enough that the propositions can be checked.

**D2 domain_accuracy (mandatory).** Claims should agree with what is known in safety-filter and safe-RL work, prior work should be represented as it actually is, and domain terms (barrier function, forward invariance, filter, shield, deadlock, absorbing state) should be used with their accepted meanings. From my seat I will assess this narrowly: whether the formal objects (propositions, metrics) are defined so that they mean what the text later says they mean, and whether numbers quoted in the text agree with the archived records. Literature completeness is Reviewer 2's job; I will only flag a domain error where it changes a methodological conclusion.

**D3 argumentative_coherence (mandatory).** The central thesis must be internally consistent and each step from evidence to claim must be supported. For a paper that pairs propositions with experiments, the coherence test is whether the experiments actually test the mechanism the propositions describe, whether negative or failed hypotheses are carried through to the conclusions rather than dropped, and whether the final claims are scoped to the conditions actually tested (one environment, specific filters, specific policy families, specific seed counts). A title that asserts a general phenomenon raises the bar: the body must show it is not an artefact of one configuration, or must say so.

**D4 cross_disciplinary_relevance (high).** A reader from control, from RL, or from experimental methodology should be able to follow the definitions and the framing. From my seat the relevant sub-question is whether the experimental protocol and the statistical vocabulary are explained so that a control-theory reader who does not run paired seed studies can judge them, and whether the metrics are defined operationally rather than by name. Interdisciplinary claims (for example that a control-theoretic property produces a learning-theoretic failure) must be substantiated by the experiments, not asserted.

**D5 writing_and_structure (normal).** Organisation, clarity, figure and table quality, and conformity to venue conventions. For me this reduces to: can the methods be reconstructed from the text plus supplement; are figures legible at printed size and do they carry the uncertainty they should (intervals, per-seed points, N); does a single table carry the weight a 10-page paper with nine-plus experiments puts on it; and are experiment and policy names consistent between text, figures, table and supplement.

## Scoring Plan

### D1: methodology_rigor
- dimension_id: D1
- what_to_look_for: a stated pre-registration (where, when, what was fixed before data: hypotheses, pass thresholds, N, seeds, analysis); paired evaluation analysed with a paired test (exact binomial / McNemar on discordant pairs for binary outcomes) rather than an unpaired test on means; the number of seeds per condition and whether per-seed results are shown, not only pooled rates; the number of episodes per cell and whether N is constant across cells; the number of hypotheses tested overall and whether a multiplicity policy (family-wise correction, pre-specified primary vs secondary, or an explicit "no correction, here is why") is stated; effect sizes (rate differences, risk ratios) with confidence intervals, not only p-values; evaluation seeds distinct from training seeds; the operational definitions of every outcome metric with thresholds and the step at which they are evaluated; declared deviations from the registration; an archive with code, records, commit hashes, and a name-to-record crosswalk; the assumptions of the two propositions stated precisely enough to be checked.
- what_triggers_block: any one of: (i) a headline numerical claim that is not supported by a test at all, or whose reported test is inappropriate for the design in a way that could flip the conclusion (for example an unpaired test on paired episodes used to claim significance, or a "significant" result at a sample size where the exact test cannot reach the threshold); (ii) a number in the text that disagrees with the archived record on spot-check in a direction that strengthens the claim, with more than one such mismatch or a single mismatch on a headline number; (iii) selective reporting: registered hypotheses or experiments that exist in the archive but are absent from the paper without declaration; (iv) no reproducibility artefacts at all for a paper that rests its credibility on registration and archiving.
- what_triggers_warn: any one of: three or fewer seeds per family without per-seed reporting or without an acknowledgement of what three seeds can and cannot establish; many hypotheses tested at a nominal p < 0.05 with no stated multiplicity policy; effect sizes without intervals, or intervals without the N they rest on; metric definitions that are given by name only (no threshold, window, or step rule); minor spot-check mismatches (rounding, one typo) that do not change any conclusion; registration asserted but not auditable from the artefacts; proposition assumptions stated informally so that the result's scope cannot be pinned down.

### D2: domain_accuracy
- dimension_id: D2
- what_to_look_for: operational definitions of the formal objects (barrier function, filter, invariance, stall / deadlock / absorbing state) that are consistent with their standard meanings and with each other across the paper; the two propositions stated with hypotheses and conclusions that the proof or proof sketch actually delivers; numbers quoted in prose, table and figure captions agreeing with each other and with the archive; prior work characterised in a way that the experiments then build on correctly (I will defer the completeness of the literature to Reviewer 2).
- what_triggers_block: a formal statement (proposition, definition) that is false as written, or whose stated conditions do not deliver its conclusion, and on which an experimental conclusion depends; or a systematic disagreement between quoted numbers and the archive.
- what_triggers_warn: a definition used in two incompatible senses between sections; a proposition whose scope is wider in the prose than in its statement; a prior-work characterisation that the methods then contradict; isolated numerical inconsistencies between text, caption and table.

### D3: argumentative_coherence
- dimension_id: D3
- what_to_look_for: a traceable chain from mechanism (propositions) to experiments to conclusion; each numbered experiment mapped to a hypothesis and each hypothesis to a pass/fail outcome; failed or undetermined hypotheses stated as such in the results and carried into the discussion; conclusions scoped to the tested conditions (environment, filter type, policy family, seed count); the title's claim matched by the body; no result that is explained post hoc by a mechanism the paper has not tested; no circularity between how a metric is defined and what it is then used to prove.
- what_triggers_block: a central conclusion that is contradicted by a result the paper itself reports; or a claim of generality (across filters, across policy families, "learning cannot remove it") that the presented experiments do not test and the paper does not qualify.
- what_triggers_warn: a failed or undetermined hypothesis reported in results but absent from the abstract or conclusion; conclusions phrased more broadly than the scope statement supports; a post-hoc explanation presented with the same confidence as a tested one; a metric whose definition is tuned to the remedy under test.

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance
- dimension_id: D4
- what_to_look_for: definitions of the statistical procedure (paired design, exact test, discordant pairs, what a "pass" means) given in the text or supplement so a control reader can follow; definitions of the control objects (filter, barrier, invariance) given so an RL reader can follow; experiment and policy names that a first-time reader can decode without the archive; the claimed bridge between the control property and the learning failure substantiated by at least one experiment that isolates it.
- what_triggers_block: the interdisciplinary core claim (a control-theoretic structure causes a learning-side failure) rests on an experiment that cannot distinguish it from a simpler explanation, and the paper does not acknowledge this.
- what_triggers_warn: statistical or control vocabulary used without definition in a way that an adjacent-field reader would need the archive to decode; naming schemes that require a crosswalk to follow the main text; an interdisciplinary implication stated in the discussion without a supporting result.

### D5: writing_and_structure
- dimension_id: D5
- what_to_look_for: methods reconstructible from text plus supplement; figures legible at printed width with axis labels, units, N and uncertainty (intervals or per-seed points) visible; the single table carrying the per-experiment N, test, effect and outcome; consistent names between text, figures, table, supplement and archive; a protocol section proportionate to a 10-page limit; venue conventions (PMLR format, page limit, reference style) observed.
- what_triggers_block: figures or table unreadable or mislabelled to the point that a result cannot be verified from them; or a methods description so incomplete that the study cannot be reconstructed even with the supplement.
- what_triggers_warn: one or more figures whose text or markers are too small at printed size to read the values the prose relies on; missing uncertainty in a figure that the text uses for a statistical claim; naming inconsistencies between sections; a protocol section whose length is not justified by what the results later need from it.
