14.1 The Journey: From Method Paper to Systematic Study

When we started (S87), the plan was clear: "Self-Play Stabilization Under Partial Observability." A method paper with positive results.

By S104, the data had spoken:

  • EMA: 1/7 success (not reproducible)
  • Pop-FSP: 0/5 success (structurally impossible)
  • G&I repro: Confirmed fragile

The pivot: A method paper would overclaim. A systematic study makes the failures central contributions.

Key decision (D40, S104): "Paper framing: 'systematic study' not 'method paper.' EMA is seed-dependent (1/4 success). A method paper would overclaim. A study paper makes EMA fragility a finding and the Pop-FSP ∀/∃ asymmetry a central theoretical contribution."

14.2 Title

"A Study of Self-Play Stabilization Methods for Competitive Deep RL in PO Pursuit-Evasion"

Why "study":

  • We systematically evaluated multiple methods (EMA, Pop-FSP, curriculum, freeze/unfreeze, etc.)
  • We report both positive (EMA seed 0) and negative (Pop-FSP, EMA seeds 1-6) results
  • We contribute a theoretical finding (∀/∃ asymmetry)
  • We verify a baseline (G&I reproduction)

14.4 Key Contributions

Contribution 1: EMA Characterization

What: First systematic evaluation of EMA opponent smoothing in competitive PE.

Finding: Shows promise (seed 0: 0.698 robustness) but poor reproducibility (1/5 with corrected LR).

Paper role: Partial positive result — EMA helps but does not guarantee convergence.

Contribution 2: ∀/∃ Structural Asymmetry

What: Novel theoretical insight explaining why population methods systematically fail in symmetric competitive games.

Finding: The pursuer trains ∃-like (one opponent), the evader trains ∀-like (all opponents). Population diversity delays lock-in but cannot correct the equilibrium.

Paper role: Central theoretical contribution — negative result becomes a structural insight.

Contribution 3: G&I 2024 Verification

What: Independent reproduction with 5 critical fixes and fragility analysis.

Finding: Paper result is reproducible but fragile. Oscillatory dynamics are intrinsic.

Paper role: Baseline validation — validates the environment and shows the problem is genuinely hard.

14.5 Answering the Reviewer's Questions

"If EMA doesn't work reliably, why include it?"

Answer: Because a systematic study must include both positive and negative results. EMA is a partial positive — it shows promise. Its fragility is itself a finding. Excluding it would misrepresent the state of the art.

"What is the contribution if most methods failed?"

Answer: Three contributions: (1) EMA characterization (first systematic eval), (2) ∀/∃ asymmetry (new theory), (3) G&I verification (baseline). The negative results define the boundary of what's possible.

"Why should I care about Pop-FSP failing?"

Answer: The ∀/∃ asymmetry is not specific to Pop-FSP — it applies to any symmetric population method in competitive games. This is a general theoretical insight.

14.7 Future Work

  2. Hierarchical policies: High-level strategy + low-level control
  3. Multi-agent population: Beyond 1v1 — teams and mixed games
  4. Real-world transfer: Sim-to-real with the G&I environment
