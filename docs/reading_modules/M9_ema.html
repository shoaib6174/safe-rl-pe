Module 9: EMA Opponent Stabilization

Estimated reading time: 4–5 hours | Prerequisites: Module 5 (Self-Play Theory), Module 6 (Curriculum)

Goal: Understand the EMA method, why it was tried, how it works mathematically, and what the 7-seed experiment revealed.

9.1 Motivation: Why EMA?

After 20+ sessions of failed stabilization attempts (freeze/unfreeze, adaptive ratio, exploration rewards, LR dampening), we asked: what if the problem is oscillation, not collapse?

Self-play produces oscillating capture rates because each agent reacts to the opponent's current policy. If we could smooth the opponent's policy, the oscillations might dampen naturally.

Key insight: Instead of preventing collapse through complex phase switching, smooth the opponent's policy so the learning landscape changes more gradually. This is the intuition behind Exponential Moving Average (EMA) opponent stabilization.

9.2 Mathematical Foundation

The EMA update maintains a smoothed copy of the opponent's parameters:

$$\theta_{\text{EMA}} \leftarrow (1 - \tau) \cdot \theta_{\text{EMA}} + \tau \cdot \theta_{\text{current}}$$

Half-Life Derivation

After n updates, the weight of the initial value decays as $(1 - \tau)^n$. The half-life is the number of updates needed for the weight to decay to 1/2:

$$(1 - \tau)^{n_{1/2}} = \frac{1}{2}$$

$$n_{1/2} = \frac{\ln(2)}{\ln(1/(1-\tau))} \approx \frac{\ln(2)}{\tau} \quad \text{(for small } \tau\text{)}$$

For tau = 0.001:

$$n_{1/2} \approx \frac{0.693}{0.001} = 693 \text{ updates}$$

With 200 steps per episode and updates every step, this is approximately 3.5 episodes.

Why tau = 0.001? Half-life of ~3.5 episodes means the EMA tracks opponent improvement fast enough to stay relevant, but smooths out high-frequency oscillations. Larger tau → faster tracking but less smoothing. Smaller tau → more smoothing but laggy.

9.3 The EMA Algorithm

  1. Maintain EMA copy: During training, maintain $\theta_{\text{EMA}}$ for each opponent actor
  2. Update rule: After each training step, apply the EMA update to the opponent's EMA copy
  3. Training: Current agent trains against the EMA opponent (not the current opponent)
  4. Evaluation: Compare current policy vs EMA policy vs current opponent

9.4 Robustness Metric

We define robustness as the worst-case capture rate across opponent types:

$$\text{Robustness} = \min(\text{CR}_{\text{random}}, \text{CR}_{\text{greedy}}, \text{CR}_{\text{selfplay}})$$

This ensures the policy isn't exploiting a weakness in one opponent type.

The LR Discovery (S98)

Seeds 0-2 used actor_lr = 0.0001 (from the initial launch script). Seed 2 only achieved robustness = 0.148. We hypothesized the LR was too low.

Seeds 3-6 used actor_lr = 0.0005. Seed 3 improved to 0.292 but still collapsed. Seeds 4-6 all collapsed.

Key finding: EMA helps (seed 0 is genuinely good), but it is not sufficient to guarantee convergence. The success rate is 1/7 (1/5 with corrected LR). This is not reproducible enough for a primary paper contribution.

9.6 Analysis: Why Did Seed 0 Succeed?

We don't know. Possible factors:

  1. Initialization luck: Seed 0's initial weights may have landed in a basin conducive to stable co-evolution
  2. LR interaction: LR = 0.0001 may have been "just right" for seed 0's landscape
  3. Curriculum timing: The annealing schedule may have aligned with seed 0's learning dynamics

What we do know: the other seeds collapsed in different ways (evader-dominant, pursuer-dominant, NaN), suggesting the instability is fundamental and EMA only delays it.
