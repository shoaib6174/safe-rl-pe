Module 11: Evaluation and Experimental Rigor

Estimated reading time: 3–4 hours | Prerequisites: Module 6 (Curriculum), Module 9 (EMA)

Goal: Understand our evaluation metrics, the checkpoint sweep methodology, and the BR exploitability analysis framework.

11.1 Capture Rate (CR)

The simplest metric: what fraction of episodes end in capture?

$$\text{CR} = \frac{\text{episodes with capture}}{\text{total episodes}}$$

Variants

VariantDefinitionWhen to Use
CR_randomCR vs random opponentTests basic competence
CR_greedyCR vs greedy opponentTests against simple but effective heuristic
CR_selfplayCR vs itselfTests self-play balance
CR_emaCR vs EMA opponentTests against smoothed opponent

11.2 Competitive Ratio

The competitive ratio normalizes capture rate to [0, 1]:

$$\text{CR} = \frac{\text{CR}_{\text{pursuer}}}{\text{CR}_{\text{pursuer}} + \text{CR}_{\text{evader}}}$$

Note: CR_pursuer + CR_evader may exceed 1 because both can capture/escape in different episodes. In practice, we compute CR as the pursuer's capture rate alone when the evader is the opponent.

11.3 Robustness

Robustness is our primary metric for policy quality:

$$\text{Robustness} = \min(\text{CR}_{\text{random}}, \text{CR}_{\text{greedy}}, \text{CR}_{\text{selfplay}})$$

Why min? A policy that achieves 90% vs random but 10% vs greedy is exploiting random's weakness. The minimum ensures the policy is strong against all opponent types, not just one.

11.4 Oscillation Amplitude

We measure the standard deviation of CR over the last 20 checkpoints:

$$\text{Oscillation} = \sigma(\text{CR}_{t-19}, \text{CR}_{t-18}, \ldots, \text{CR}_t)$$

High oscillation (>0.2) indicates instability. Low oscillation (<0.05) suggests the system has settled.

11.5 Checkpoint Sweep Methodology

A checkpoint sweep evaluates all saved checkpoints to understand the training trajectory:

  1. For each checkpoint (every 250 episodes), load the policy
  2. Run 100 evaluation episodes against 3 opponent types (random, greedy, self-play)
  3. Record CR for each opponent type
  4. Compute robustness = min(CR_random, CR_greedy, CR_selfplay)

For a 5000-episode run with checkpoints every 250 episodes: 20 checkpoints × 100 episodes × 3 opponents = 6000 evaluation episodes.

11.6 BR SAC Exploitability Analysis

To test whether a claimed "Nash equilibrium" is actually stable, we train a best-response (BR) agent against the frozen checkpoint:

  1. Freeze the policy at checkpoint M2550 (claimed NE point)
  2. Train a fresh SAC agent as pursuer against frozen evader
  3. Train a fresh SAC agent as evader against frozen pursuer
  4. Compare BR performance vs baseline performance

Hypotheses

HypothesisConditionInterpretation
H1Both BRs fail to improveGenuine Nash equilibrium
H2One BR improves, other doesn'tPartial equilibrium — one side exploitable
H3Both BRs improve significantlyNeither side is at equilibrium

Result (S88)

For SP17b seeds 48 and 49 at M2550:

SeedBR Pursuer CRBR Evader CRVerdict
48≤ 0.115 (no improvement)0.41–0.52 (gap +0.08–0.10)H3 — evader exploitable
49≤ 0.115 (no improvement)0.41–0.52 (gap +0.08–0.10)H3 — evader exploitable
Conclusion: Both seeds showed H3 — the evader was exploitable. The "equilibrium" was not a true Nash. This finding led to the paper's reframing as a "systematic study" rather than a method claiming convergence.

11.7 Tournament Evaluation

For population methods, we use round-robin tournament evaluation:

  • Each population member plays against every other member
  • Record win/loss matrix
  • Compute Elo ratings or similar
