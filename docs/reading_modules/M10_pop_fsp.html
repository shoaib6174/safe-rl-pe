10.1 Fictitious Self-Play (FSP)

Fictitious Self-Play is a classic game-theoretic algorithm. Instead of training against the current opponent, each agent trains against a mixture of all past opponent policies.

The FSP Algorithm

  1. Maintain a population of historical opponent policies
  2. Sample an opponent from the population (uniform or weighted)
  3. Train best response against that sampled opponent
  4. Add the new policy to the population

The intuition: by facing diverse opponents, the agent learns robust strategies that generalize.

10.4 The ∀/∃ Structural Asymmetry Finding

After 5 failed configurations, we asked: is the problem in the hyperparameters, or is it fundamental?

The Formal Argument

Consider the symmetric game where both agents use identical architectures and training procedures. Define the objectives:

  • Pursuer: ∀ opponent policies π_e ∈ Π, maximize capture rate
  • Evader: ∃ opponent policy π_p ∈ Π, escape from capture

Wait — these are not symmetric. Let's be more precise:

  • Pursuer's training objective: Given a population of evaders, learn a policy that captures as many as possible. Formally: max_π_p E_{π_e ~ pop}[CR(π_p, π_e)]
  • Evader's training objective: Given a population of pursuers, learn a policy that escapes as many as possible. Formally: max_π_e E_{π_p ~ pop}[1 - CR(π_p, π_e)]

These look symmetric, but the structure of the game introduces asymmetry:

The ∀/∃ Asymmetry:
  • The pursuer needs to be good against one opponent at a time (sampled from population). Its objective is ∃-like: "find a strategy that works against this opponent."
  • The evader needs to be good against all pursuers in the population. Its objective is ∀-like: "find a strategy that escapes from ALL of them."

Why ∀ Is Harder Than ∃

In machine learning terms:

  • ∃ (pursuer): Binary classification — does this strategy beat this opponent? One positive example is enough.
  • ∀ (evader): Multi-class coverage — does this strategy escape all opponents? Must handle every opponent type.

The pursuer's learning problem is easier because it can specialize against the specific opponent it faces. The evader's problem is harder because it must generalize across all pursuer styles.

Implications

  1. Population methods cannot fix this without explicit architectural asymmetry (e.g., larger evader network, different learning rate, curriculum favoring evader)
  2. Speed/ratio tuning only shifts the equilibrium point, not the structural problem
  3. The asymmetry is intrinsic to symmetric competitive games where one side must handle all opponent strategies

10.5 Why v6 Oscillated Instead of Collapsing

Pop-FSP v6 (arena=19, evader=1.65×) was interesting: it did not fully collapse. Instead, CR oscillated around 0.72.

Why? The larger arena and evader speed advantage partially compensated for the ∀/∃ asymmetry. The evader had enough room and speed to occasionally escape, preventing total lock-in. But the equilibrium was still biased, not balanced.

Insight: Population diversity delays lock-in (~3× longer in v7 vs v6) but cannot correct the equilibrium point. The structural asymmetry pushes the system toward pursuer-dominance regardless of diversity.
