### The premise check FAILED — the denser reward never materialised
Spec §4 required recording the realised reward density precisely so this could be checked
rather than assumed:

| | predicted (from the clone's audit) | realised in training |
|---|---|---|
| 400-step arms | 485 positives / 1M | **37 / 1M** |
| 1200-step arm | 184 / 1M | **11 / 1M** |
| ratio | 2.6× | **3.4×** |
| training capture fraction | 0.158–0.179 | **0.013–0.015** |

The **ratio** held — truncation does densify the signal by roughly the predicted factor. The
**absolute level** came in 13× below prediction, because the truncation table was computed
from the *clone's* rollouts and the policy stopped being the clone within 50,000 steps of the
actor unfreezing. A collapsed policy captures almost nothing, at either horizon.

So the round did not test "a learner at a denser reward". It tested "a learner that collapses
before the density can matter".

### Design limitation — mine, in the specification, not in the run
The spec fixed the learner as `finetune_br` (LayerNorm critics, `ent_coef = 1e-5`, 50k
warm-up), calling it the proven recipe. That was a mistake in framing. Those are fixes for
specific earlier bugs (D80/D81); the resulting **unanchored** configuration is precisely the
one D82 diagnosed as collapsing when the actor leaves the clone under a drift-dominated
critic, and Tier 0's CTRL reproduced that at 0.010. The only variant that ever held its ground
was the **anchored** one (T0.4 RB at β = 1.0 preserved its score; T0.5 RE held ≥ 0.137 for a
full 1M steps).

**I selected a learner with no headroom to improve.** Every arm was already collapsed at the
first probe — 100k steps, i.e. 50k after the actor unfroze — which is before any horizon
effect could plausibly operate.

**What this round therefore establishes, stated narrowly:** a shorter episode does **not**
rescue a collapsing learner, and the collapse is indifferent to the horizon (HA at 400 and HC
at 1200 are indistinguishable, both 0/500 final). **What it does not establish:** whether a
shorter horizon would let a *stable* learner improve — the hypothesis as posed in spec §1.
That question remains open and this round did not fairly test it.
