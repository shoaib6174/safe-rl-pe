### T3 exact-game twin — criterion MET on 3/3 layouts (commit bab287a)
7×7 gridworld from Experiment 1 with hazard cells and a projection shield; exact Nash value and BR dynamics; pre-registered threshold |ΔV| > 0.02 on ≥ 2/3 layouts.

| layout | unshielded | both shielded | Δ both | pursuer-only | Δ pursuer |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sparse | 0.5748 | 0.1362 | −0.4385 | 0.1274 | −0.4474 |
| dense | 0.5603 | 0.0575 | −0.5029 | 0.0575 | −0.5029 |
| corridor | 0.5032 | 0.6127 | **+0.1096** | 0.1898 | −0.3134 |

- **Pursuer-only shielding is uniformly negative** (−0.31 to −0.50): the clean signed **R1 prediction**, and exactly the Step-8 asymmetry confound.
- **Both-shielded flips sign with geometry**: scattered hazards favour the evader, a chokepoint favours the pursuer. Jia et al. (CEP 2026, D90) assert the mechanism; this is its first measurement, and the direction dependence is new.
- Controls: hazards placed where no policy moves, and the hazard-free arm, both give Δ = 0.0 exactly; every configuration passes a unilateral-deviation saddle check with gap 0.0; both arms of a layout share identical hazards and starts, so only the projection differs. A second projection rule gives bit-identical values (only the projection's image matters to an exact optimiser), so the shift is not an artefact of the "nearest" definition.
- **Caveat for the paper**: magnitudes belong to a small grid where the shield removes much of the pursuer's mobility; the SIGN transfers, the size does not. Sanity check passes: pursuer-only is never better for the pursuer than both-shielded.
- Incidental: BR dynamics reach a 5-cycle, not a fixed point, on every layout.

### T4 Step 6 shield gates — KILL CRITERIA FAIL (commits 94c9690…2db8555; 416 pe2 tests; `results/visual_gates/step_06/step_06_gate.json`, fingerprint b72fe438…)
Run exactly as pre-registered in S119 (N=1000 per arm, seeds 760000+/770000+), plus G5 (D87 robust margin δ = 0.05 m, provenance phase4 §8.7 + `test_localization_accuracy`).

| gate | criterion | result |
|---|---|---|
| G1 shielded main | 0 violation steps / 1000 eps | **FAIL** — 519 steps, 16 eps |
| G2 capture intact | shielded CR ≥ 0.5 × unshielded, McNemar | PASS — 0.428 vs 0.415, p = 0.019, ΔCR +0.013 |
| G3 shielded vs `ObstacleHuggingEvader` | 0 violation steps / 1000 eps | **FAIL** — 72 steps, 17 eps; timeout 0.690 |
| G4 negative control (unshielded probe) | ≥ 5 % eps with violation | PASS — 74.4 %; probe needed no iteration |
| G5 robust margin δ = 0.05 | G1 + G3 criteria | **FAIL** — 56 main + 1283 probe steps; intervention 0.788 vs 0.706 |

**The violation census separates two mechanisms, and they need different fixes.**
- **Mechanism A — float-scale wall grazing (479 of G1's 519 steps).** Overshoot p50 5.8e-12 m. Heading ≈ π/2 makes cos θ ≈ 0, so the virtual control point gives no lead against the wall; a barrier of −6.9e-10 violates the DCBF row by 5.4e-10, **below the solver's `FEAS_TOL = 1e-9`**, so the nominal passes unchanged and the env's clip fires on a 1.3e-10 m step. Structurally the body cannot cross the limit (crossing needs cos θ > 0, where the VCP is strictly tighter). A tolerance-scale artifact, but a genuine failure *as pre-registered*, and both candidate fixes (the solver tolerance; the magnitude-blind violation definition) touch a pre-registered constant.
- **Mechanism B — real centimetre penetration on the relaxed fallback tier.** 32 obstacle contacts in G1 at p50 1.2 cm, max 4.7 cm; all of G3's 72 above 0.33 mm, max 5.0 cm; `min_clearance` reaches −0.0997 ≈ −d. Chain: the DCBF row is a first-order linearization whose neglected term at ω_max is ≈ 1 mm/step; a barrier driven slightly negative makes the exact QP infeasible (at v = ω = 0 every row is γh ≥ 0, so infeasible ⟺ some h < 0); **the relaxed tier then drops the obstacle constraints entirely**, so the shield offers no obstacle protection exactly when an obstacle barrier has gone negative, and the pursuer drives in until the env projects it out. **G5 makes this 25× worse**: inflating radii shrinks the feasible set, infeasibility rises 0.000055 → 0.001525 on the probe, the relaxed tier is entered 1261× vs 18, and the arm parks inside an obstacle at p50 penetration 5.0 cm = δ. The robust margin buys earlier warning at the cost of far more frequent recourse to a fallback with no obstacle protection.

This is the shield's own metrics (D89: infeasibility, tier histogram, clearance floor) doing what they were built for. Mechanism B is a **design defect in the fallback tier**, not a tolerance question. Visual gate `step_06_shield.gif` signed (C1–C4, md5 1fe0e9de… on two renders) on its own terms only.

## Three interpretation / design questions — the user's
1. **Does PA's kill rule count as fired?** Literally yes (35 < 50). By the pre-registered breakdown's stated purpose, no: frozen-phase 80 > 50, then collapse. Recommendation: the breakdown governs; record the collapse as the finding, not sparsity.
2. **Is PB a survivor?** By the pass rule yes; by the pre-registered growth condition no. Recommendation: "preserved, undetermined as evidence of learning" — which is precisely what A4 pre-committed β=0.1 as the follow-up for.
3. **Step 6 shield: two changes, both pre-registration-touching.** (a) Mechanism B is a design fix: the relaxed tier must not drop obstacle constraints — relax the *decay rate* or add per-constraint slack (weighted so obstacles outrank boundary), and consider a second-order or margin-corrected DCBF row to absorb the ≈ 1 mm/step linearization error. (b) Mechanism A is a definition question: either the violation definition gains a physical tolerance (e.g. clip magnitude > 1e-6 m) or `FEAS_TOL` drops below the clip's numerical floor; either must be re-pre-registered and G1/G3/G5 re-run on fresh seeds. Recommendation: fix (a) first as a proper redesign (it is the substantive defect and the one hardware will hit), then re-pre-register (b) with the tolerance stated in metres, then re-run all five gates on new seed ranges.
