### Root Cause Assessment

The structural asymmetry in Pop-FSP training:
- **Pursuer trains with ∃**: catch ANY evader from population → easier
- **Evader trains with ∀**: escape ALL pursuers from population → harder

This ∀/∃ asymmetry is **fundamental**. Game modifications (arena size, speed ratio) can shift the equilibrium but cannot eliminate the asymmetry. Population-based sampling tweaks (recency bias, asymmetric populations, gradual mixing) compensate partially but insufficiently.

**Conclusion**: Pure Pop-FSP with game modification alone cannot reliably produce competitive balance in this 1v1 pursuit-evasion domain. Additional mechanisms are needed (e.g., explicit balance enforcement, adaptive reward shaping, or alternative training architectures).
